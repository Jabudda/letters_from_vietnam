# Letter 012: notes

## Source

Two sheets torn from a spiral notebook (red rules, a margin line, torn holes along the top), written in blue ink; three pages, not numbered. Addressed "Dear Dad, Mom & Kids", dated in the text "(its June 13 or so)". A water stain covers the lower half of page 2, which he left blank ("this pen can't write over water"). Signed "Your son & brother, Tom", with a decorative flourish below it, and a P.S.

The airmail envelope is addressed in his hand to the "Jafuta Family" in West Allis, Wisconsin, with the return address "T. Jafuta, HHB 3/6 ARTY, APO SAN FRAN 96318" and "Free" (torn at the right edge). "6/13" and a small scribble are written on the front in darker ink, perhaps by someone at home. There is no postmark. On the back, a few figures are written sideways at the left edge (perhaps "31" and "80").

## Open questions for the family

1. **The year.** "Today I got about 64 days or so (its June 13 or so)", and "my new ETS date is moved up to August 21st". Counting 64 days from 13 June gives 16 August, close to his 17 August port call in Letter 011. Letters 005, 010 and 011 count to the same few days. So this is very likely **13 June 1969** (interpretation), a week before Letter 005. Confirm?
2. **The ETS date.** "I know that I told you my new ETS date is moved up to August 21st now." He says he wrote this before; that letter may be lost or not yet found. Does the family know when his service ended?
3. **"We sure were busy."** He apologizes for not writing "for about the last week" because "we sure were busy", underlining "were". Letter 005 (19 or 20 June) says he had just left Ben Het. Nothing in this letter says where he was; does the family know?
4. **The check from Dad.** "One from Dad too - with the check)". Fine to publish?
5. **Who wrote "6/13" and the scribble on the envelope?** Letter 010's envelope is marked "6/24" the same way. Were these written by Grandma or Grandpa when the letters arrived? And the figures on the back?
6. **Readings to check:** the crossed-out word before "June 13"; "One from Dad too" (page 1); the crossed-out word and "Opps" at the top of page 2.

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "Today I got about 64 days or so (its June 13 or so)"; "my new ETS date is moved up to August 21st now"; "for 60 more days I could put up with anything".
- **Historical context:** ETS (Expiration of Term of Service) is the date a soldier's term of active service ends. *Sources:* [AFVN veterans' glossary, "E"](https://www.afvnvets.us/rel_viet-dict-e.html); [University of Virginia, "Sixties Project" glossary of Vietnam War terms, D–J](https://www2.iath.virginia.edu/sixties/HTML_docs/Resources/Glossary/Sixties_Term_Gloss_D_J.html).
- **Interpretation:** His count of days in Letters 005, 010, 011 and this letter all point to the same few days in mid to late August 1969: a port call on 17 August and an ETS date of 21 August.
- **Historical context:** Ben Het was surrounded by North Vietnamese regiments from mid-June 1969 (see Letter 005 notes and sources). This letter, written about 13 June, does not mention Ben Het or any action, and nothing here places him at any event.
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "I guess the mail system is a bit mixed up... Late letters are a hell of alot better then no letters." Letter 010 also complains of mail not going out or coming in.
