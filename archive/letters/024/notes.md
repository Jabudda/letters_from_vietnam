# Letter 024: notes

## Source

One sheet of lined notebook paper (blue rules) written on both sides in dark blue ink, in large, careful handwriting for a small boy. Addressed "Hi Petey". Undated. Signed "Your brother, Tom", with a decorative flourish, then "Write soon & be good." Lower on the second side is a note to his parents: "Think this letter is to deep for Petey."

The plain envelope is addressed in his hand to **"Petey Jafuta (Jr.)"** in West Allis, Wisconsin, with "Free" and the return address "T. Jafuta, HHB 3/6 ARTY, APO SAN FRAN 96318". There is no postmark. Only the front was scanned. The master folder is labelled "Letter 24_Petey".

### Reading edition: changes from the diplomatic text

- Line breaks joined into paragraphs, following his indented paragraph starts. Page markers and the envelope are kept in the diplomatic text only.
- The note to his parents is marked as such in italics, since it sits apart from the letter to Petey.
- The flourish under his signature is left out; it is in the diplomatic text.
- His spelling, grammar and punctuation are unchanged, including "Are you going to have a party.", "How's your girl friends?", "powed", "your" for "you're", "POW", "By for now" and "to deep". The word in quotation marks, which reads like "bishing", is kept as it appears (Petey's own word for fishing, per Andy).

## Open questions for the family

1. **The date.** "Pretty soon is your birthday" (Petey's birthday is in August, Andy, 2026-11-10), "Maybe the army will be nice and let me come too", "when I get home". So this was written in early or mid-summer, very likely **June or July 1969**, near the end of his second tour (interpretation). It is the second letter to Petey in the collection: Letter 018's letter to Petey also says "I'll miss your birthday Petey". Which came first?
2. **"Petey Jafuta (Jr.)".** Petey was named for Grandpa (Peter). The obituaries name him Peter; the letters always call him Petey, and so did the family: "We always called Dad's brother, my uncle Petey" (Andy, 2026-11-10).
3. ~~**"bishing".**~~ **Answered (Andy, 2026-11-10):** it means fishing; "Petey likely called it Bishing." Dad put Petey's own word in quotation marks.
4. **Petey's barber shop.** "How's your barber shop. You and me will go get a haircut when I get home!" Letter 018 also mentions "your barber shop". Which barber shop, and did they go? Andy is not sure (2026-11-10); still open for other relatives.
5. **"Did you get powed lately??" and "POW".** A family word for a spanking?
6. **The plane ride.** Letters 018 and 019 also promise Petey a plane ride. Did it happen?
7. **"Too deep for Petey".** Did Petey understand it? Andy (2026-11-10): "Petey likely does not remember the letters as they have been in storage for 50 years."
8. **Readings to check:** "powed"; "Then I'll buy you a cake" (or "coke"? Letter 018 has "buy a coke"); "to deep".

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** Written from HHB 3/6 ARTY, APO 96318, to his little brother before Petey's birthday, while Dad still expected to be in the army then ("Maybe the army will be nice and let me come too").
- **Interpretation:** With Petey's birthday in August and his ETS of 21 August 1969 (Letter 012), this belongs to the summer of 1969.
- **Unknown:** The exact date and where he was.
