# Letter 044: notes

## Source

Thin airmail paper folded in a block, written in dark blue ink on one side, six pages. The third and fifth pages are numbered "2" and "3" in circles (on the fold above each photographed panel); the others are not numbered. Each page runs on from the one before ("a speeding / ticket"; "I worry / enough to make up for a 100 people"; "And for once / in my life"; "No, I'm / not going to be a no-it-all"). Addressed **"Dear Mom"**; not dated, but it answers Grandma's letter "dated June 20th". Signed "Your ol' TIRED TOO son, Tommy" ("TIRED TOO" written in capitals above the line), with a flourish, and a P.S. No envelope was photographed.

**Source images.** These are the family's phone photographs (HEIC), taken with the letter in a clear plastic sleeve. `Letter_002_Page_05` and `_06` are second photographs of `_03` and `_04` and are not used in the reading order. The folder is labelled "Letter_044_June20_DaNang_Mom", taken from the earlier typed draft, `Letter 044 Transcription.docx`, which read "Da Nang" where the page reads **"Ben Het"**. That draft paraphrases and was used only as a second reader.

### Where this transcription differs from the earlier draft

The earlier draft paraphrases and misreads several key words. The most important: it reads **"you asked where Da Nang is. Just a small plane ride from Saigon"**, where the page reads **"you asked where Ben Het is. Just a small place 7 mile WEST from Dak To here"**, and "Especially since we live up in [unclear]" where the page reads "Especially since we been up in Ben Het". It reads "That food pouch" for "That foot powder"; "the 40-day mark – age that is" for "the 40's - not my age but 40 days"; "I wrote a letter to Betty too" for "I wrote a letter to Petey too"; and "Glad I took life for granted then" for "Used to take life for granted Mom. But not anymore."

### Reading edition: changes from the diplomatic text

- Line breaks joined into paragraphs, following his indented paragraph starts. Page markers are kept in the diplomatic text only.
- Words he wrote above the line ("WEST", "then", "TIRED TOO") are put in their place. His underline of "near" is shown in italics. A doubled dash at a line break ("good - / - real") is shown once.
- His spelling, grammar and punctuation are unchanged, including "'em", "no where", "we been", "then" for "than", "Cause to stop your worrying", "alot", "hay", "no-it-all" and "Tommy".

## Open questions for the family

1. **The date.** Grandma's letter "dated June 20th" arrived "today"; "in about a couple more days or so I'll be in the 40's… days" (with an ETS of 21 August, 49 days left fell on 3 July); the phone call is past ("my previous letter", Letter 025, about 21 June). So this is very likely **about 30 June 1969** (interpretation). Letter 047, to Dad, has the same NBC filming "today" in its second part (about 30 June) and ends "I'll close for now & write a letter to Mom": this is very likely that letter, written the same evening.
2. **"Especially since we been up in Ben Het… Its really bad up there now Mom & I sure worry about Deacon & all the rest up there."** Dad, back at Dak To, writes of Ben Het, where he had been with Deacon on 18 June (Letter 034). The siege was lifted on about 29 June and declared over on 2 July 1969.
3. **The news at home (an answer for the family's question in Letter 025).** "There's probably a big thing about it back home. But there's more to it then meets the eye… I don't know what the paper & news say about it back home & don't really care as long as you know I'm OK. Thats why I have been writing so much lately. Cause to stop your worrying." Dad knew the family would be hearing about Ben Het in the news and wrote often so they would not worry. See Letter 025 notes, "News reports at home", including the AP story of 27 June 1969 about A Battery, 3/6 Artillery.
4. **NBC at Ben Het or Dak To.** "Today some NBC news men were taking pictures of us air lifting ammo so like I told Dad, if you see a familiar face on the news guess who? I'm for CBS myself." Letter 005 (to Dad) says "If you watch the CBS news you may get lucky and see me", and Letter 025 says ammunition was to be "air lifted to Ben Het to our Battery". Did anyone at home see Dad on the news? The Vanderbilt Television News Archive may list NBC Evening News reports on Ben Het for the end of June 1969.
5. **The phone call and the speeding ticket.** "Sure am glad you didn't get a speeding ticket though even though it would have been worth it to hear your voice again." Did Grandma race home for the MARS call (Letter 025)?
6. **"I wrote a letter to Petey too. How's he like it?"** Very likely Letter 024, written to Petey before his August birthday (interpretation).
7. **"The help I gave to these people in this messed up country."** What did Dad mean? Does the family know?
8. **Readings to check:** "foot powder"; "'em (our feet)"; "7 mile WEST"; "a 100 people"; "no-it-all".

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** Grandma had asked where Ben Het was; Dad answered "Just a small place 7 mile WEST from Dak To here", writing from the Dak To area after being "up in Ben Het". NBC newsmen filmed his unit "air lifting ammo".
- **Historical context:** Ben Het lay about 15 km (about 9 miles) northwest of Dak To. *Source:* [Wikipedia, "Ben Het Camp"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Het_Camp). Dad's "7 mile WEST" is his own estimate.
- **Historical context:** The siege of Ben Het was front-page news in the United States in late June 1969, and the press warned that the camp might be overrun; the enemy withdrew on about 29 June and the siege was declared over on 2 July. *Sources:* [Time, "The Lesson of Ben Het", 11 July 1969](https://time.com/archive/6637066/world-the-lesson-of-ben-het); [Project CHECO, *The Siege of Ben Het* (DTIC ADA487237)](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA487237.pdf). See Letter 025 notes.
- **Unknown:** Whether the NBC film was broadcast.
- **Historical context (NARA, 2026-11-10):** The Army's award file describes the siege in detail: 434 rounds of indirect fire on Ben Het between 23 and 26 June, a sapper attack on Battery A, 3/6 Arty on 23 June, and the NVA withdrawal on 27–28 June after bombing close to the perimeter. It also confirms Ben Het is "approximately 13 km west of Dak To" (Dad: "7 mile WEST"). See `research/ben-het-1969-records.md`.
