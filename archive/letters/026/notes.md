# Letter 026: notes

## Source

Three sheets of thin airmail paper written in blue ink, six pages. Pages 2, 3 and 5 are numbered in circles and page 4 has a faint "4"; pages 1 and 6 are not numbered. Each page runs on from the one before. Addressed "Dear Dad, Mom & Kids" and dated in the text "Today is June 7th". Signed "Love & miss, Tom", with a decorative flourish. The last line of page 4 runs off the bottom edge of the scan.

The plain envelope, marked with dark smudged rings, is addressed in his hand to the "Jafuta Family" in West Allis, Wisconsin, with "Free" and the return address "T. Jafuta, HAB [for HHB] 3/6 ARTY, APO SAN FRAN 96318". There is no postmark. At the left, written sideways in blue ink, are some figures and a doodle in another hand (perhaps a sum; meaning unknown). Only the front was scanned.

## Open questions for the family

1. **The year.** "Today is June 7th", "about 74 days left in the army", and his ETS "moved up to the 21st of August instead of the 29th". 7 June plus 74 days is 20 August. So this is very likely **7 June 1969** (interpretation). Confirm?
2. **This is the letter that first gave the new ETS date.** Letter 012 (about 13 June 1969) says "I know that I told you my new ETS date is moved up to August 21st". This letter is very likely the one he meant (interpretation).
3. **The PX catalog pages.** "I'm sending a few pages out of this PX catalog… send the money order out of my money and that will be my present to you on your anniversary in Sept." Letter 010 (24 June) asks whether they got "the PX catalog pages", and Letter 008 mentions the Super 8 movie camera. Did the pages survive? What did Grandma and Grandpa choose for their anniversary?
4. **Grandma and Grandpa's anniversary.** He says it is "in Sept." (Letter 015 had suggested it fell "in or near October".) Can the family confirm the date? (Only if wanted in the book.)
5. **"Help our 'A' baty out."** "We have to go up around Dak To again tomorrow near Ben Het to help our 'A' baty out. They got 22 men in the hospital since yesterday morning so need replacements." Does the family know whether he went, and for how long? (His next letters, 005 and 025, have him back from Ben Het by 19–20 June.)
6. **Deacon, Joker, Harry and Den.** "At least we'll get to see Deacon out there"; "I wonder how Joker is doing now. Getting shorter"; "ol' Harry just took the dumb monkey"; "Bet [possible reading: Den] will get drafted pretty soon". Who were they? Deacon, Joker and Harry sound like army friends or nicknames; Den may be someone at home (Dennis, of "Pat & Dennis" in Letter 019?).
7. **The pets in the hooch.** "3 dogs, 1 monkey & a wounded cat." Was Snoopy one of the three dogs (Letters 002, 006, 008, 016)?
8. **Readings to check:**
   - "tied by my [possible reading: cot]" (page 1);
   - the last line of page 4, which runs off the scan ("[possible reading: a gorilla]");
   - "[possible reading: Den]" (page 5);

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** On guard "the other night" he fired an M79 round at movement on the perimeter, "about 100 yards with just a full moon to go on". In the morning they found a wounded cat.
- **Historical context:** The moon was full on 31 May 1969 and at last quarter on 7 June, so a night lit by a nearly full moon fits the week before 7 June 1969. *Sources:* [Catalina Sky Survey (University of Arizona), moon phases, June 1969](https://catalina.lpl.arizona.edu/moon-phases/month/1969-06); [Calendar-12, dates of moon phases in 1969](https://www.calendar-12.com/moon_phases/1969).
- **Historical context:** The M79 was a single-shot 40 mm grenade launcher. (General knowledge; his own words, "a m 79 round… with the grenade launcher", say the same.)
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "We have to go up around Dak To again tomorrow near Ben Het to help our 'A' baty out. They got 22 men in the hospital since yesterday morning so need replacements." This is his report; it has not been checked against unit records.
- **Historical context:** A platoon of A Battery, 3/6 Artillery, was at Ben Het in 1969, and Ben Het was surrounded by North Vietnamese regiments from mid-June (see Letter 005 notes and sources). Cpl Harold F. Hawkins of A Battery was wounded on 13 June 1969 (Letter 005 notes). Nothing in this letter places Dad in any particular engagement.
- **Historical context:** An AP story from Ben Het three weeks later (Lancaster *Daily Intelligencer Journal*, 27 June 1969) reported that A Battery, 3rd Battalion, 6th Artillery, on "North Hill" with six self-propelled 105 mm guns, had passed 100 casualties in two weeks and was at times at only half strength (see Letter 025 notes, "News reports at home"). Nothing here says how long Dad was with A Battery.
- **Unknown:** Where he was when he wrote (not at Ben Het; he was to go "up around Dak To again tomorrow").
- **Cross-reference (from later letters):** Letter 034 (18 June 1969) has Dad at Ben Het with Deacon ("We're still up in Ben Het yet"); Letter 047 (about 28 June) has him at Dak To airlifting ammunition to A Battery, which "almost got over run". John left for another unit on 2 June (Letter 040).
