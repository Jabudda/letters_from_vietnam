# Letter 047: notes

## Source

Thin airmail paper folded in a block, written in dark blue ink on one side, five pages; two panels carry page numbers in circles on the fold. Addressed **"Dear Dad"**, to Grandpa alone. Not dated, but it counts **"54 days (Aug. 21st)"** to his ETS. Written over two days: a second part begins **"2 days later, Well back again Dad."** Signed "Your son, Tom", with a flourish. No envelope was photographed.

### Where this transcription differs from the earlier draft

The earlier draft reads "up to now it's terrible" where the page reads **"up in Ben Het its terrible"**; "our BC from 'C' battery… our battery… C battery alone" where the page reads **"A baty"** each time; "(Hoff)" where the page reads **"(Acoff)"**; "up near 30" where the page reads "up near 50"; "The 75's we drag in so good that it's even hard for the NVA to get them" where the page reads "The NVA are dug in so good that its even hard for air strikes to get them!"; "the 1st and 9th" for "the ARVN's"; and "some B-52s (rockets)" for "some ROK (KOREANS)". Its date label, 1968, is also wrong.

## Open questions for the family

1. **The date (the folder label is wrong).** "these are going to be a long 54 days (Aug. 21st)". Fifty-four days before 21 August is **28 June**. His ETS of 21 August was set in June 1969 (Letter 026, 7 June 1969), and the letter is about Ben Het under siege, so this is **about 28 June 1969**, with the second part "2 days later", **about 30 June 1969** (interpretation, a strong one). It is not from 1968.
2. **Letters 047 and 044 were written the same day (a cross-reference).** The second part of this letter says "today some NBC news guys were there and got pictures of us air lifting ammo" and "I'll close for now & write a letter to Mom". Letter 044, to Mom, says "Today some NBC news men were taking pictures of us air lifting ammo so like I told Dad, if you see a familiar face on the news guess who?" So Letter 044 is very likely the letter to Mom he mentions, written the same evening, about 30 June 1969 (interpretation).
3. **"A good friend of mine (Acoff) died."** Very likely SP4 Eddie Duane Acuff of HHB, 3/6 Artillery, killed on 23 June 1969 in Kontum province, the "Eddie Acoff" of Letter 018 to whose parents Dad wrote (interpretation; Andy has approved publishing his name). Dad spells it "Acoff". **Andy (2026-11-10): "Acoff is Eddie Acoff."**
4. **"They almost got over run 2 days ago again… Now the casualties are up near 50 for A baty alone."** Dad's own account, from Dak To, of A Battery, 3/6 Artillery at Ben Het in late June 1969. An AP report printed on 27 June 1969 said the battery's casualties had "passed the 100 mark" in two weeks (Letter 025 notes); the figures differ, and neither has been checked against unit records. **This is the closest thing yet, in Dad's own words, to a report of his battery being overrun.**
5. **"Even our BC from A baty broke down 2 days ago."** The battery commander. The AP report names Capt. John Horalek as A Battery's commander in late June 1969 (Letter 025 notes); whether he is the "BC" Dad means is not known.
6. **"Don't worry Dad, I'm not going to sign up again."** Had Grandpa asked, or worried that he might? Andy (2026-11-10): "Dad was a little unpredictable so Grandpa maybe thought he would sign up for a third tour." (Andy has said Dad went back for a second tour to be with his friends; this letter is near the end of that tour.)
7. **"I was hoping you'd mention something about the dog in this letter."** The hunting dog he hoped to get when home (Letters 025, 035, 038, 041, 013).
8. **"I bet you felt the same way hay Dad. I mean when you were in the army."** Grandpa was a World War II veteran.
9. **Readings to check:** ~~"(Acoff)"~~ confirmed (Andy, 2026-11-10: "Acoff is Eddie Acoff"); "up near 50"; "ROK (KOREANS)"; the page numbers on the fold.

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** About 28 June (1969, interpretation) he was "still in Dak To helping to air lift ammo & food to our baty" at Ben Het, and two days later he and others were "humping alot of ammo out to A baty"; NBC newsmen filmed the ammunition airlift.
- **Historical context:** Ben Het was surrounded by North Vietnamese regiments in June 1969; the road was cut and supplies came in by air. The enemy withdrew on about 29 June and the siege was declared over on 2 July 1969. *Source:* [Project CHECO, *The Siege of Ben Het* (DTIC ADA487237)](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA487237.pdf).
- **Historical context:** A Battery, 3rd Battalion, 6th Artillery had guns at Ben Het (Fire Support Base 12) in mid-1969. *Source:* [1/92nd Artillery, Operational Report for the period ending 31 July 1969](https://www.bravecannons.org/History/ORLL/j_may69-jul69.html). See Letters 025, 026 and 034.
- **Historical context:** SP4 Eddie Duane Acuff, HHB 3/6 Artillery, was killed on 23 June 1969 in Kontum province. *Sources:* [The Virtual Wall](https://www.virtualwall.org/da/AcuffED01a.htm); [Vietnam Veterans Memorial Fund, Wall of Faces](https://www.vvmf.org/Wall-of-Faces/175/EDDIE-D-ACUFF/page/2/).
- **Interpretation:** "Up in Ben Het its terrible" and "a guy gets scared" are Dad's words from these days; they fit Letter 034 (18 June, "We're still up in Ben Het yet") and Letter 044. Nothing here places him in any particular engagement.
- **Historical context (NARA, 2026-11-10):** The Army's award file for Dak To–Ben Het records that at 0315 hours on 23 June 1969 eight NVA sappers assaulted the perimeter of Battery A, 3d Battalion, 6th Artillery on Ben Het's North Hill. Seven were killed; one satchel charge went off between howitzer sections 1 and 2, and a grenade in a perimeter bunker killed one man and wounded two. Dad, writing about 28 June, says they "almost got over run 2 days ago again". "2 days ago" points to about 26 June, and NVA ground forces stayed close around Ben Het until they were driven off on 27 June. "Again" suggests an earlier attempt, and the attack of 23 June fits it (**interpretation**). The records do not name the man killed in the bunker on 23 June, the same day Eddie Acuff died, so whether it was Eddie is **unknown**. Battery A was later named in the Valorous Unit Award for Dak To–Ben Het (General Orders 5022, 1970). See `research/ben-het-1969-records.md`.
- **Historical context:** The President's Daily Brief of 28 June 1969 told President Nixon that the Communists "stepped up their shelling of Ben Het on the night of 26 June". (CIA, released in part.)
