# Letter 038: notes

## Source

Three large sheets of lined paper (blue rules), written in black ink on one side, six pages. The pages are numbered "II" and "III" (Roman numerals) and then "4" and "5" in circles; pages 1 and 6 are not numbered. Each page runs on from the one before. Addressed "Dear Dad, Mom & Kids" and dated in the text "today is July 25th". Signed "Your ol' son & brother, Tom", with a flourish, and "Oh say 'hi' to all - thanks". The last sheet has its lower left corner cut off; most of it is blank. There is no envelope in this folder.

### Reading edition: changes from the diplomatic text

- Line breaks joined into paragraphs, following his indented paragraph starts. Page markers are kept in the diplomatic text only.
- "mak- / ing" (split at a line end) joined. Words he wrote above the line ("you", "kids") are put in their place. His underline of "out" in "without" is shown in italics.
- Crossed-out words and the flourish under his signature are left out; they are in the diplomatic text.
- His spelling, grammar and punctuation are unchanged, including "hay", "alot", "then" for "than", "befor their hatched", "nothing may not have ever happened", "talk too", "make me think" and "By, by for now".

## Open questions for the family

1. **The date.** "today is July 25th so that leaves only 6 days left in this month"; "I'll leave my unit the 20th of Aug. so that leaves 26 days left in ol' 3/6"; and "I hear that they landed on the moon safe and also got back to earth". Apollo 11 landed on 20 July 1969 and came back on 24 July. So this is **25 July 1969** (verified by the moon landing and the day count).
2. **His eyes.** "I went to the Doctor about my eyes. But he couldn't do any good or figure it out." Letter 023 (16 July 1969) says he had had eye trouble since Ben Het. Did he see an eye doctor after he came home? What was wrong?
3. **"Maybe tomorrow" (my ol' saying).** Does the family remember Dad saying it?
4. **"Fishing with Dad, Mick & I come next month"; "when I teach Dad how to catch muskies."** Did they go? (Letter 001 has "a true fish story"; Andy has said Dad was an avid fisherman.)
5. **The hunting dog.** "What's the story on a good ol' hunting dog?????? Or are you trying to discourage it. You know who that 'you' is don't you Mom?" Compare Letters 025, 035 and 013 (the puppies and the new pup).
6. **"Except for when I was home that one time… I just wanted to give up that day."** Dad does not say what happened, and nothing here explains it. It is in his own words and is kept exactly; the family decides how it is presented in the book (see Privacy review). **Decided (Andy, 2026-11-10): published as written** ("Publish everything, no censorship."; "Please publish the remaining letters.").
7. **"The day I reach the top is when I'll have my own family."** Andy was born in November 1970, about fifteen months after this letter.

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** On 25 July 1969 he expected to leave his unit (3/6 Artillery) on 20 August, with "a couple days left in the army at the most" after that (his ETS was 21 August, Letter 026). He had been "pulling guard and junk like that", and that night was "elected to go walk down on the perimeter".
- **Historical context:** Apollo 11's lunar module landed on the Moon on 20 July 1969, and the crew splashed down in the Pacific on 24 July 1969. *Sources:* [NASA, "Triumph After Splashdown of Apollo 11"](https://www.nasa.gov/?p=416229); [Britannica, "Apollo 11"](https://www.britannica.com/explore/space/apollo-11/).
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "It'll feel alot better this time getting off that plane and stepping on American ground. I can still remember how happy & thankful I was last time knowing that I would probably come back over before I got out of the army." His own account of coming home after his first tour knowing he would probably return.
- **Unknown:** Where he was when he wrote.
