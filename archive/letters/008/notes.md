## Open questions for the family

1. **The year.** He clearly wrote "May 10, 1966", but the letter itself says "Jim & I were one of their first replacements. Ours unit got here in '66 & we did in '67", so it was written in 1968 or later. The clues point to **1969** (interpretation): he writes of his time in Vietnam "both times", so he is on his second tour; he is "in Ben Het", where Letter 005 (June 1969) also puts him; he has lost Jim's address and asks for it again, as he does in Letter 006 (about July 1969); "Snoopy" also appears in Letters 002 and 006; and "M.T.C." may be the name Milwaukee Technical College carried from 1968 to 1970 (see Historical notes). One thing to weigh: Letter 003 is dated 11 May 1969 and says "We had to go to the Oasis yesterday", which would be the same day as this letter. Could both be right (for example, "we" meaning his battery rather than himself)? Letter 010 (very likely 24 June 1969) also asks whether the family got "the one where I sent prices about a movie camera... pages taken out of a PX catalog", which is what this letter promises to send, so this letter came before it. Does the family know the year?
2. **Snoopy and the "Gaines burgers".** "Oh, Snoopy also. The Gaines burgers I mean. I was dipped 'em in in my gravy for him. He sure did like it." The family seems to have sent dog food for Snoopy. Was Snoopy a dog Dad kept in Vietnam? (Letter 002 mentions Snoopy too.)
3. **Jim.** "Remember when you sent his address way back when? Well, I wrote to him and never got an answer." And "Jim & I were one of their first replacements" in '67. Is this Jim Theilke (the envelope back of Letter 006)? Did Jim come home safely?
4. **Who are Dave/Davey, Gene Plummer and Ken?** "That beer party at Dave's house"; "By now, Davey should be over here"; "ol' Gene Plummer... lost alot of weight"; "go see Ken (on guard) Its his 21st birthday today". Does the family know them? May their names be published?
5. **"M.T.C." and "a month drop".** "About M.T.C. Yes, it will almost be a month drop. Its just the idea of feeling obligated that I don't care for." Was M.T.C. Milwaukee Technical College, and did he plan to leave the Army about a month early to start school there? (Interpretation; see Historical notes.)
6. **"Before I was discharged."** "That part where you said - 'before I was discharged' really made me feel good." Does the family know what this refers to?
7. **The Super 8 movie camera.** Did he buy one from the PX? Do any of his home movies survive?
8. **The April $200 pay check.** He asks whether it arrived and how much he has saved. Fine to publish? (It is ordinary money talk, not account details.)
9. **Readings to check:** "I didn't feel too good" (page 1); "Snoopy"; "Gaines burgers"; "I was dipped 'em in in my gravy"; "(or I" (page II); "Our (my) time will come" (page III); "Im sure all will work out" (end of page III); "Ben Het" (page V); "Ours unit" (page VI).

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** He and Jim arrived in Vietnam in 1967 as some of the unit's first replacements, and the unit had arrived in 1966: "Ours unit got here in '66 & we did in '67."
- **Historical context:** The 3rd Battalion, 6th Artillery arrived at Pleiku in June 1966 (see Letter 004 notes and sources), which agrees with "Ours unit got here in '66".
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** He writes that at present "we're in Ben Het, a bit past Kontum", that An Khe is "about 35 miles or so" from him and that he has been there "quite a few times", that "Our main HQ is in Nha Trang", and that he has been in "every place possible in the Central Highlands".
- **Historical context:** I Field Force, Vietnam, the corps-level command over US Army forces in the Central Highlands, had its headquarters at Nha Trang, and its field artillery groups included the 52nd Artillery Group, to which 3/6 Artillery belonged. *Sources:* [I Field Force, Vietnam (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Field_Force,_Vietnam); [Order of Battle, I Field Force Vietnam (ichiban1.org)](https://www.ichiban1.org/html/iffv_ob.htm).
- **Interpretation:** He passed through Nha Trang each time he arrived in and left Vietnam and on his way to R&R ("Just before I came here both times & for R&R... And also when I left both times"). "Both times" means this is his second tour.
- **Historical context:** Gaines-Burgers were a dog food launched by General Foods in 1961: individually wrapped, moist patties shaped like hamburgers that needed no refrigeration. *Sources:* [Gaines-Burgers (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaines-Burgers); [Duke University Libraries, Gaines-Burgers TV advertisement, 1960s](https://repository.duke.edu/dc/adviews/dmbb47317).
- **Historical context:** In Vietnam-era Army slang, a "drop" was a cut in a soldier's remaining time in service, and an "early out" was a discharge before the scheduled date. The Defense Department also had a directive (dated 5 January 1968) on early release of enlisted soldiers to enroll in college or vocational/technical school. *Sources:* [University of Virginia, "Sixties Project" glossary of Vietnam War terms, D–J](https://www2.iath.virginia.edu/sixties/HTML_docs/Resources/Glossary/Sixties_Term_Gloss_D_J.html); [AFVN veterans' glossary, "E"](https://www.afvnvets.us/rel_viet-dict-e.html); [DoD Directive 1332.15 history (GlobalSpec)](https://standards.globalspec.com/std/131867/dod-d-1332-15).
- **Historical context:** Milwaukee Area Technical College was called Milwaukee Technical College from 1968 to 1970. *Source:* [MATC Alumni, list of the college's former names](https://www.matc.edu/alumni).
- **Interpretation:** "About M.T.C. Yes, it will almost be a month drop" may mean he planned to leave the Army almost a month early to start at Milwaukee Technical College, and that he disliked feeling "obligated" by it. This is unconfirmed; Letter 003 says he was weighing college against a plumbing apprenticeship.
- **Unknown:** The exact year and his precise location on the day he wrote. Nothing here places him at any particular action.
