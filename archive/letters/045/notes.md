### Where this transcription differs from the earlier draft

The earlier draft reads "best wishes to little Lee" where the page reads "best better be"; "I got much happiness today. I took property to the E.O.C." where the page reads "Not much happened today. I took Snoppy to the Doc"; "exchange malfunction" where the page has a skin complaint ("[possible reading: red mange]… whatever that is"); "Ol' Snoppy didn't care for it" as "I probably didn't care for it"; and "I left Dear Mom's address" where the page reads "Well dear family". The letter is about **Snoopy**, Dad's dog in Vietnam.

### Reading edition: changes from the diplomatic text

- Line breaks joined into paragraphs, following his indented paragraph starts. Page markers are kept in the diplomatic text only.
- "Snoppy" is read as **Snoopy**, the family's name for Dad's dog (Andy, 2026-11-10; as in Letters 025, 029 and 031). The spelling as written is kept in the diplomatic text.
- A crossed-out word and the flourish under his signature are left out; they are in the diplomatic text.
- His spelling, grammar and punctuation are unchanged, including "best better be", "He say it", "junk", "allright" and "2½ ton".

## Open questions for the family

1. **The date.** "the day after good ol' Thanksgiving", "hoping I'll see you next month". Letter 043 (Thanksgiving Day, very likely 28 November 1968) plans the December R&R in Hawaii and the visit home. So this is very likely **Friday 29 November 1968** (interpretation, a strong one), and the two letters are a pair.
2. **Snoopy in November 1968.** "I took Snoppy to the Doc cause he has developed a slight case of the '[possible reading: red mange]' whatever that is… Ol' Snoppy didn't care for it but its for his best." Snoopy was already Dad's dog early in the second tour; he is named again in Letter 031 (23 December 1968) and through June 1969 (Letters 039, 027, 025). Who was "the Doc"? Andy (2026-11-10): "Doc was a Doctor in the family and the joke was that he was blind in one eye and could not see out of the other." (In this letter Dad and Snoopy were in Vietnam, so "the Doc" who treated Snoopy there may have been someone in the unit, such as a medic, who shared the family nickname or the joke; the letter does not say. Interpretation; please confirm.)
3. **The 2½ ton truck.** "I didn't know the first thing about it but learned quickly after grinding a few gears… once I got into 5th gear I was allright." His first time driving one. Did he drive trucks again later?
4. **Readings to check:** "[possible reading: same]" (page 1); "[possible reading: red mange]"; the crossed-out word on page 2; the red pen mark.

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** The day after Thanksgiving (1968, interpretation) he took Snoopy to "the Doc" and drove a 2½ ton truck for the first time.
- **Historical context:** The "deuce and a half" 2½-ton cargo truck was the U.S. Army's standard medium truck in Vietnam (general knowledge; not checked against a source here).
