# Letter 033: notes

## Source

Six sheets torn from a spiral notebook (red rules, a red centre line, torn holes along the top), written in black ink on one side, six pages. Pages 3, 4 and 5 are numbered in circles; the others are not. Undated. Addressed "Dear Dad, Mom & Kids". Signed "Your son & brother, Tom", with a decorative flourish, and a P.S.

**Reading order.** The scans are in the order 0169, 0170, 0171, 0172, 0173, 0174, but the text runs 0169 → 0172 → 0171 (3) → 0170 (4) → 0173 (5) → 0174: "Their / small things to many people"; "He will / someday though - the hard way"; "So I really don't / consider him too lucky"; "Nothing like home / cooked meals"; "but I write / how I feel".

The plain envelope has a faint printed block at the top (unreadable) and a rust-coloured stain at the left. It is addressed in his hand to the "Jafuta Family" in West Allis, Wisconsin, with "Free" and the return address "T. Jafuta, HHB 3/6 ARTY, APO SAN FRAN. 96318". There is no postmark. Only the front was scanned.

## Open questions for the family

1. **The date.** Undated. He has been "back here" for a while (after his December 1968 visit home, Letter 031); it is "getting cold out here at night"; the monsoons have not come; it "must finally be warming up" at home; and he is waiting on M.T.C. about his acceptance (as in Letter 032, 17 January, and Letter 019, 24 April). So this is very likely **February or March 1969** (interpretation). Can anyone narrow it?
2. **Chuck and Chuckie (an important cross-reference).** "Glad to hear Chuck made out Ok on the car… I sure hope that Chuckie sticks out high school. They really should make that part of his probation. Chuck should check into that… Ol' Chuckie just doesn't realize that." Here **Chuck** and **Chuckie** seem to be two people: Chuck, a grown man, and Chuckie, a boy of high-school age. Andy has said Chuckie was Grandma's brother, and Grandma's obituary names her brother Charles Collins. This letter suggests instead that **Chuck may have been Grandma's brother (Charles Collins) and Chuckie his son, Dad's cousin** (interpretation). The same worry appears in Letter 016 (March 1969): "I just don't know if that guy will ever learn. Someday he will - the hard way." Which is right?
3. ~~**Privacy: "his probation".**~~ This mentions a young relative's probation. **Answered (Andy, 2026-11-10):** "Publish everything, no censorship. Thank you for checking." The line stays in full.
4. **The mail.** "One from Mom, 1 from Dad, one from Tom [possible reading: Kulinski], one from John [possible reading: Danbeck] & one from Tim." Who were Tom and John, and can anyone read their surnames? Is Tim the cousin Tim Collins (see Letter 020)?
5. **Petey and the soda at Jack's.** "Petey remembers me buying him a soda at Jack's." Where was Jack's? (Letters 018 and 024 also promise Petey a soda or "a cake" and a trip to "your barber shop".)
6. **Mary at D.Q.** "I'm real glad to hear that Mary got a job at D.Q." Dairy Queen (interpretation)? Which one?
7. **"You all liked [possible reading: Snoppy]."** Did Dad send home pictures of Snoopy? (Andy: Dad's Vietnam dog was Snoopy.)
8. **"The guy… from 35th and Capitol."** A Milwaukee corner (35th Street and Capitol Drive). Did Dad keep in touch with him?
9. **The camera.** "Just take some money out of my check and get the kind I asked for." (See Letter 032 on "one of those little 35mm cameras".)
10. **Readings to check:** "[possible reading: Kulinski]" and "[possible reading: Danbeck]" (page 1); "Jack's"; "D.Q."; "Blackhawk"; "[possible reading: Snoppy]"; the order of pages.

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "We went out to Blackhawk yesterday to escort a 'low boy' with a 155 gun in… Yesterday when we came back from Black hawk (near An Khe) we stopped at a village to watch a Buddist ceremony - the out door type." A "low boy" is a low-bed trailer for hauling heavy equipment; a 155 is a 155 mm artillery piece (general knowledge).
- **Unknown:** The exact place he calls "Blackhawk" ("near An Khe"), to be checked against sources.
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "Our B.C. got mad at us cause we broke the window on the jeep… the 60 turned over and broke the window." (B.C. = battery commander; the 60 = the M60 machine gun.)
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "I'm sorry about some of my letters but I write how I feel and when depressed I write bad letters. But one thing I'd like to stress is that I don't ever feel sorry for myself." (Compare Letters 004 and 032.)
