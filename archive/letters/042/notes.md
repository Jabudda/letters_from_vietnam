# Letter 042: notes

## Source

Four sheets of thin, grey-blue airmail paper, written in black ink on one side, seven pages. Pages 3 to 6 are numbered in small circles and page 2 has a faint number at the top (possible reading: 2); pages 1 and 7 are not numbered. Addressed **"Dear Mom"**, to Grandma alone, and dated in the text "today is Aug. 8th" (the figure may be written over a 9). Signed "Your ol' tired mixed up - but HAPPY son, Tom", with a flourish. In it Dad calls it **"the last letter I'll be writing"**.

**Reading order.** The scans run 0222, 0223_a, 0223_b, 0224, 0225, 0226, 0227, 0228, but the text runs 0222 → 0224 (2) → 0223_b (3) → 0226 (4) → 0225 (5) → 0227 (6) → 0228: "But not good enough. / Its been getting pretty active"; "When I hear your / voice on the phone though I'll be / the happiest guy"; "The parents were just beam- / ing with joy"; "Do you think / it will?"; "Thats what / makes life such a challenge"; "God bless / you." **0223_a** is the back of the sheet scanned as 0223_b: it is blank, and the writing on it is the page 3 text showing through the thin paper.

The envelope is the printed "UNITED STATES ARMED FORCES - VIETNAM" kind with a map of Vietnam. It is addressed in his hand to **"June Jafuta"** (Grandma by her own name, as on Letter 021) in West Allis, Wisconsin, with the return address "T. Jafuta, 3/6 ARTY, APO SAN FRAN 96318" and "Fr[ee]" (the rest torn away at the right edge). There is no postmark. Only the front was scanned.

## Open questions for the family

1. **The date.** "today is Aug. 8th" (possibly 9th), "5 days and a wake up left in good?? ol' 3/6 and about 8 days left in the army", the POW release in the paper (5 August 1969), and the "big wedding anniversary this year". So this is **8 (or 9) August 1969** (interpretation, a strong one). Letter 035 (29 July) had him perhaps going home on 18 August on another man's port call; "5 days and a wake up" from 8 August is about 14 August.
2. **"The last letter I'll be writing."** Is this the last letter Dad wrote home from Vietnam? Letter 013 (6 August 1969) is two days earlier.
3. **The phone call home.** "When I hear your voice on the phone though I'll be the happiest guy in the world. I hope I don't cry though… Thats why you shouldn't get worried if I don't call on the date we planned. There's a 99% chance I will." Does anyone remember the call and the day he came home? (Letter 035's envelope has "19th of August, after 3:00" in pencil.)
4. **"About 24 yrs. of marriage."** Grandma and Grandpa's anniversary in September (Letters 026, 039). 1969 minus 24 is 1945. The family records (Grandma's obituary) say they were married for 65 years; Grandpa died in July 2011. Can the family give the year of the wedding?
5. **Petey's birthday.** "I'm kinda mad at myself for forgetting his birthday." Petey's birthday is in August (Andy). Letter 024 was written to Petey before it.
6. **"34 or so months" of letters.** Since about September 1966, which fits "30 months ago I raised my right hand" in Letter 036 (25 March 1969).
7. **His eyes.** "Also I been running around getting my eyes checked out too." See Letters 023 and 038.
8. **Readings to check:** "Aug. 8th [possible reading: 9th]"; the faint page number on page 2; "[possible reading: if]" on page 5.

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** He was pulling guard "every other nite", had "5 days and a wake up left in… ol' 3/6", and said it had been "getting pretty active 'again'" after "that 6 week quiet spell".
- **Historical context:** On 5 August 1969 North Vietnam released three American prisoners of war, Navy Lieutenant Robert Frishman, Seaman Douglas Hegdahl and Air Force Captain Wesley Rumble, to a visiting delegation of American antiwar activists; their return was widely reported. Dad's "picture in the paper on a mother and father who heard that their son, a POW was released" very likely refers to this (interpretation). *Sources:* [Wilson Center, "Treatment of American POWs in North Vietnam"](https://wilsoncenter.org/publication/treatment-american-pows-north-vietnam); [*The Press* (Christchurch), "Good Treatment In Hanoi", Reuter, 7 August 1969](https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/newspapers/CHP19690807.2.115).
- **Historical context:** The release mattered beyond the families. One of the three, Seaman Douglas Hegdahl, had memorized the names and details of about 256 fellow prisoners and brought them home, and on 2 September 1969 the Defense Department held a press conference at which two of the released men described the torture and mistreatment of American prisoners in North Vietnam. Treatment of the prisoners improved markedly that autumn. *Sources:* [Naval History and Heritage Command, H-Gram 043-2, "Sojourn Through Hell"](https://history.navy.mil/about-us/leadership/director/directors-corner/h-grams/h-gram-043/h-043-2.html); [Wilson Center, "Treatment of American POWs in North Vietnam"](https://wilsoncenter.org/publication/treatment-american-pows-north-vietnam). (Added at Andy's suggestion, 2026-11-10.)
