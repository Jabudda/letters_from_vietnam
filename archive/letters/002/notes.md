## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** He writes that he has been "out here at the Oasis for the last 3 or 4 days" and doesn't know how long they will stay. He describes red dust "about ankle deep", says the dust blows over him "when the guns go off", and that the worst place to sleep is "on muddy sand bags or a muddy hole".
- **Historical context:** Landing Zone Oasis (also LZ Tuttle) was a U.S. Army base about 24 km southwest of Pleiku, just south of Route QL-19. It was built in 1965, reopened in May 1966, and was in use until about 1971; units based there included the 3rd Brigade, 4th Infantry Division, and several artillery and armor units. Its airstrip is listed with a laterite surface. *Source:* Wikipedia, "Landing Zone Oasis" (article and infobox), accessed 2026-11-10.
- **Interpretation:** Letter 001 places him "back in Pleiku with my unit", so "the Oasis" is very likely LZ Oasis. The letter does not name the base fully, and his unit is not stated, so this is not confirmed.
- **Interpretation:** Laterite is a red soil, which fits his joke that "we all look like red heads (red dust, out here)".
- **Interpretation:** "When the guns go off dust blows all over you" suggests he was close to artillery positions. The letter does not say which guns or what unit.
- **Interpretation (date):** Andy confirms Grandpa's birthday is January 13. Page 7 says the family wrote that "the birthday card came on time" and asks what kind of cake Grandpa had, so the birthday had just passed. A letter dated "the 22nd" nine days after January 13 fits **22 January** best. A February date is possible if mail was slow, but less likely.
- **Historical context:** In the Pleiku area the dry season generally runs from about November to April, with the rains from about May to October. *Source:* Local Vietnam, "Pleiku – Travel Guide & Things To Do" (general climate summary; a modern travel source, not a 1960s record).
- **Interpretation:** A January date falls in the dry season, which fits his complaint that the dust is "about ankle deep". He also mentions mud, so the weather may have changed recently or he is remembering the wet season.
- **Verified fact (family):** Grandpa's birthday was 13 January, and he turned 49 in January 1969 (family records, supplied by Andy).
- **Interpretation (year):** Three things point to **1969** rather than 1970. (1) Grandpa turned 49 in January 1969 (family records), and Tom writes "another 49 (or 50) years", leading with 49. (2) Letter 001, dated 21 July 1969, has Tom counting down to leaving his unit in mid to late August 1969; a January 1970 letter would need him back in Vietnam after that, which nothing so far suggests. (3) Letter 001 mentions "last year before I left", and this letter speaks of having been home and come back, which fits a home visit before January 1969. Andy confirmed 22 January 1969 on 2026-11-10.
- **Unknown:** Nothing in the letter ties it to a particular operation or event, and it should not be linked to any event at the base without other evidence.
