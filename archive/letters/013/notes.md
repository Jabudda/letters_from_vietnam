# Letter 013: notes

## Source

Three sheets of thin blue-grey airmail paper written in black ink, five pages. Addressed "Dear Dad", dated in the text "Today is Aug 6", with the 6 circled. Pages 2 to 4 are numbered in a circle. On page 3 the number is written over the word "believe" in the first line. Signed "Your ol' son, Tired Tommy" (the last word is uncertain), with a decorative flourish below it. Then come a postscript about pictures and the pup, "See you soon" over a column of short strokes, and "12 DAYS AT THE MOST (HOPEFULLY) Nothing is *for sure* in the army. Love, Tom" in capitals and script.

**Reading order differs from scan order.** Scan 0069 is page 3 and scan 0068 is page 4. His circled page numbers show this, and so do the sentences: page 2 ends "its almost over with" and scan 0069 continues "It sure is hard to believe though"; scan 0068 ends "I just can't" and the last page begins "wait."

The envelope is the printed "UNITED STATES ARMED FORCES - VIETNAM" kind, with a map of Vietnam that marks Pleiku, An Khe, Qui Nhon, Nha Trang and other places. It is addressed in his hand to "Peter Jafuta" (his father) in West Allis, Wisconsin, with "Free" and the return address "T. Jafuta, 3/6 ARTY, APO SAN FRAN 96318". There is no postmark. Only the front was scanned.

## Open questions for the family

1. **The year.** "Today is Aug 6", "About in 4 more days I'll stop writing. Cause 7 days from then I should be home", "12 DAYS AT THE MOST". Two days after Letter 011 (Aug. 4, port call Aug. 17), so very likely **6 August 1969** (interpretation). Confirm?
2. **The pup.** Grandpa wrote "you know what. About the pup", and Dad guesses he "built a run way kennel the length of our garage", offering to pay half "out of my 'fat' bank roll". What was the new dog's name? Any photos?
3. ~~**Fritz.**~~ Answered: Grandpa named all his dogs Fritz; they were German Shorthairs (Andy, 2026-11-10). Was the new pup a Fritz too?
4. **Hunting and fishing.** "We got alot of hunting & fishing to do." Did he and Grandpa hunt and fish together after he came home? Where?
5. **"Your machine."** Answered in part: Grandpa owned and ran Pete's Plumbing Service (Andy, 2026-11-10), so "business" means the plumbing business. What was the machine in the pictures?
6. **His apprenticeship.** "I'll be happy when I start my apprent. some day." Grandpa ran Pete's Plumbing Service, and Andy says that is where Dad was later fired from. Did Dad apprentice with Grandpa? When did he start?
7. **Grandpa's service.** "The army probably hasn't changed much since you been in hay Dad?" Grandpa served in World War II (see the home page). Is this the first letter that speaks of it?
8. ~~**The signature.**~~ Answered in part: Dad also called himself Tommy (Andy, 2026-11-10), which supports "Tired Tommy". The reading stays marked until the family checks the scan.
9. **Privacy.** "Thats why when ever we get hit I feel 'relieved' in a certain sense." This is a strong line about the strain of waiting. Publish as written?

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "It has been very quiet (too quiet) lately but things are starting to pick up again"; "when ever we get hit I feel 'relieved' in a certain sense"; "All these 'lifers' are getting shook up with all the intell. reports coming in lately so they got us working at all hours."
- **Historical context:** "Lifer" was soldiers' slang for a career soldier, often used in a derogatory way. *Source:* [University of Virginia, "Sixties Project" glossary of Vietnam War terms (K–P)](https://www2.iath.virginia.edu/sixties/HTML_docs/Resources/Glossary/Sixties_Term_Gloss_K_P.html).
- **Interpretation:** The letter names no place, unit action or date of any attack, and nothing here places him at any particular event. "Things are starting to pick up again" and the intelligence reports are his only words on it.
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "The rains are starting to break up a bit... the mud gets to me after awhile."
- **Historical context:** Pleiku and the Central Highlands have a rainy season from about May to October, wettest around July and August, under the summer monsoon. *Sources:* [Climates to Travel, "Pleiku climate"](https://climatestotravel.com/climate/vietnam/pleiku); [VnExpress International, "A travel guide to Vietnam's Central Highlands"](https://e.vnexpress.net/news/travel/travel-guide/a-travel-guide-to-vietnam-s-central-highlands-4852001.html). (Present-day climate descriptions; the 1969 weather itself is from his letter.)
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** He planned to stop writing "in 4 more days" because he "should be home" seven days after that, about 17 August by his count. That agrees with the "Aug. 17" port call in Letter 011.
