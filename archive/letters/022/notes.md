# Letter 022: notes

## Source

Five sheets torn from a spiral notebook (red margin line, blue rules, torn holes along the top), written in blue ink on one side. The pages after the first are numbered in circles: the second sheet's number looks like a "2" written over an ink blot, the third is also numbered "2", then "3" and "4". The reading order below follows the text, which runs on from page to page ("him playing / out in the snow"; "Thats / why I asked to see Petey"; "That / is both a nice thought"; "Do / you ever look at all of us"). Addressed "Dear Mom". Undated in the text. Signed "Your ol' son, Tom", with a decorative flourish below.

The airmail envelope (red and blue border, printed "VIA AIR MAIL / CORREO AEREO / PAR AVION") is addressed in his hand, in blue ink, to **"Mrs. June Jafuta"** in West Allis, Wisconsin, with "Free" and the return address "T. Jafuta, HHB 3/6 ARTY, APO SAN FRANCISCO 96318". **It is postmarked "ARMY & AIR FORCE POSTAL SERVICE, 96318, NOV 20, 1968, APO".** There is a black ink blot at the top left. Only the front was scanned.

## Open questions for the family

1. **The date (fixed by the postmark).** The envelope is postmarked **20 November 1968** at APO 96318. He mentions Nixon, elected on 5 November 1968, and says this Christmas is "the last one I'll miss". So the letter was written in mid-November 1968, about three weeks after Letter 020 (29 October 1968). This is the second-earliest dated letter so far.
2. **"The North woods."** "By the time you get this Dad will probably be off to the North woods getting meat for our starving family." Grandpa went deer hunting "up north" in November. Where did he hunt? Is this the same "north woods" the family had been to in Letter 001? (Andy has said "up north" usually meant West Bend.)
3. **The first homecoming.** He remembers ringing the doorbell, standing and looking at the house, and asking "to see Petey right away" so he would not cry. When was that homecoming (the end of his first tour, early 1968)?
4. **The letter to Oakland.** "When I read your letter to me down in Oakland I felt like a brick wall hit me… not knowing that you knew all along." Letter 007 says the same: "I never did want to let you all know I was coming back over here but of course you found out", and mentions Mom's letter "down in Oakland". Does Mom's letter survive?
5. **Ed and the orders.** "I didn't bother to hide my orders back home… I know Ed saw them and couldn't make out nothing." Is this Ed, Kathy's fiancé (later her husband)?
6. **Jim.** "I wonder where Jim is now?? Maybe if you call his house his Mom would know." Letter 020 (29 October) expected "ol' Jim" to arrive; this suggests he had not. Is this Jim Theilke?
7. **"As we said last year - the gap is closing."** A family saying from his first tour?
8. **Readings to check:** "[possible reading: anyte] longer" (page 3); "Surprised you hay" (page 1); the number on page 2.

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (postmark):** Mailed through the Army & Air Force Postal Service at APO 96318 on 20 November 1968.
- **Historical context:** Richard M. Nixon was elected President on 5 November 1968, after promising during the campaign "to bring an honorable end to the war" in Vietnam. *Source:* [Vietnam War Commemoration, "Richard M. Nixon is Elected President"](https://vietnamwar50th.com/1968_tet_and_shifting_views/Richard-M-Nixon-is-Elected-President).
- **Interpretation:** "I wonder if ol' Nixon is going to settle this mess over here. It'll probably take another few years" fits a letter written within weeks of that election.
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "It was terribly hard for me to come back over here." This, with Letter 007 and Letter 020, places the start of his second tour by October 1968, through Oakland.
- **Unknown:** Where he was when he wrote, and where Grandpa hunted.
