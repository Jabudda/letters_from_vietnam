# Letter 031: notes

## Source

Two sheets of lined paper written in blue ink, three pages, not numbered. The letter is in two parts: it begins at Cam Ranh Bay ("awaiting a flight back to my unit") and goes on under the heading **"Dec. 23rd"** after he got back. Addressed "Dear Family". Signed "Love & miss you all, Tommy", with a decorative flourish, and a P.S. signed "Tom": "This is the year (1969) - my year."

The plain envelope is addressed in his hand to the "Jafuta Family" in West Allis, Wisconsin, with "Free" and the return address "T. Jafuta, HHB 3/6 ARTY, APO SAN FRAN 96318". There is no postmark. Only the front was scanned.

The scans are numbered 0159, 0160, 0163 and 0164 (0161 is the last page of Letter 030; there are no scans 0162 in either folder).

## Open questions for the family

1. **The date (confirmed).** "Dec. 23rd", "almost Christmas Eve", and "This is the year (1969) - my year." So the second part was written on **23 December 1968**, and the first part a few days earlier at Cam Ranh Bay. **Verified (historical record):** Pleiku Air Base was hit by 16 rounds at 01:47 on 23 December 1968, which matches "Had guard last nite and Pleiku Airbase (about 1/2 mi from us) got the hell knocked out of them" (see Historical notes).
2. **Home for four days in December 1968.** "It sure was a just 4 days but I really enjoyed it alot… the thoughts of me just being home last week seems like a wonderful dream." Two months into his second tour, Dad was home for about four days in mid-December 1968 and then flew back through Cam Ranh Bay. **Does the family remember this visit, and why he was able to come home?** (Letter 022, postmarked 20 November 1968, says nothing of a coming trip; Letter 017 says "When I landed in Hawaii (both times) and called you all", which may refer to the flights home and back.)
3. ~~**The dog's name.**~~ **Answered (Andy, 2026-11-10):** "Dad's Vietnam dog was Snoopy, but he may have been KIA." Dad's writing here looks like "Snoppy"; the family's reading is used. Earlier note: "Of course to see [Snoppy]… [Snoppy] sure was happy to see me. He just cried and jumped all over me. He lost alot of weight though." The dog he kept in Vietnam. Dad's spelling here looks like "Snoppy"; Letters 029 and 025 have a similar word ("Snappy"). Andy has said the dog was Snoopy. **Snoopy, Snoppy or Snappy?**
4. **Davey.** "I also got a letter from Davey. He should be home by now." Davey had served too (see Letters 001, 008 and 020). Who was he?
5. **"I guess Dad had that same feeling."** Grandpa was a World War II veteran. Did Grandpa talk about coming home from the war?
6. **Readings to check:** "[possible reading: keyed] up and nervous" (page 3); "a just 4 days".

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** He was home for about four days in mid-December 1968, flew back through Cam Ranh Bay, and was with his unit by 23 December.
- **Historical context:** Pleiku Air Base was attacked at 01:47 on 23 December 1968 with 16 rounds. *Sources:* [Vietnam Security Police Association, "365 Days: December" (air base attacks, 1964–73)](https://www.vspa.com/pdf/365_12_dec.pdf); [EC-47 site, Luther David's diary of rocket attacks on Pleiku, Aug 1968–Jan 1969](https://archive.ec47.com/diary.htm).
- **Interpretation:** The attack fits his "Had guard last nite… Pleiku Airbase (about 1/2 mi from us)" exactly, and puts his unit about half a mile from the air base. The 3/6th Artillery was based at Camp Saint Barbara (Artillery Hill) outside Pleiku (see Letter 004 notes and sources).
- **Historical context:** The allies had declared short Christmas cease-fires in earlier years; in 1967 it was a 24-hour pause from 18:00 on 24 December, during which many violations were reported. *Sources:* [CIA reading room, "The Situation in Vietnam – 25 December 1967"](https://cia.gov/readingroom/document/06752216); [Vietnam Helicopter Pilots Association, Army Reporter on Christmas truce violations, 1967](https://www.vhpa.org/KIA/panel/battle/67122530.HTM). Dad thought another truce a mistake: "If they have that Xmas truce this year - they're crazy." (His opinion as written. Whether and how a truce was held at Christmas 1968 is not checked here.)
- **Unknown:** Why he was able to go home in December 1968.
- **Cross-reference (from a later-numbered letter):** The four days at home were planned. On Thanksgiving (very likely 28 November 1968) Dad wrote that he was "taking my R&R to Hawaii next month on the 13th. And I plan on making it home from there… It will only be for about 4 days" (Letter 043).
