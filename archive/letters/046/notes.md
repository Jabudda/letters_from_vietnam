### Where this transcription differs from the earlier draft

The earlier draft has "pretty definite I will be changed early" for "pretty definate I will be dis- / charged early"; "if they have a summer course around then, such as a summer refresher" for "if they have a semester around then. Such as a summer refresher course"; and an ending, "Just a few notes using a pencil… if all works out next month I'll be seeing you all", that is not on this sheet (it is close to Letter 043's ending). It also gives the P.S. as "the type of course or school… attendance is required", where the page reads "what type of class I'm enrolling in. It was business law wasn't it? What ever is underlined has to be filled in by whoever is responsible".

## Open questions for the family

1. **The date (a cross-reference).** Undated. "I'm entitled up to a 90 day early out… It would bring the date up to June 25th." Ninety days before 25 June is about 25 September. In Letter 036 (25 March 1969) he wrote "Almost exactly 30 months ago I raised my right hand", that is about 25 September 1966; three years from that is about 25 September 1969. So this letter is very likely from **early 1969**, before the early out was approved (by 1 June 1969, Letter 040), probably **January to March 1969** while he was waiting to hear from M.T.C. (Letters 032, 033, 019) (interpretation).
2. **"Mr. Tom."** He signs off as "Mr. Tom", as in Letter 036 ("I walk out of Ft. Louis as a 'Mr. Tom.'").
3. **The school letter.** "It was business law wasn't it?" Was the course at M.T.C. (Milwaukee Technical College, Letters 008, 019, 032, 033)? Did Grandma get the letter, and did it go through? (The early out was approved by 1 June 1969, Letter 040, with a discharge date of 29 August, later 21 August, Letter 026.)
4. **The sample letter.** "A copy like the one here… I copied it off this reference sheet I found." Did the family keep the sample?
5. **Readings to check:** "definate"; "semester"; "business law"; the third line of the signature, "Jafuta".

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** He asked Grandma to get a letter from a school showing he was enrolling in a course, so that he could be "discharged early", up to 90 days before his date.
- **Historical context:** In 1969 the Army allowed soldiers to be released up to about 90 days before their scheduled separation date to attend school ("early out for school"), on proof of enrolment. *Source:* not yet checked against an Army regulation; general knowledge, to be confirmed.
- **Unknown:** The exact date, and whether this was the letter that got his early out approved.
