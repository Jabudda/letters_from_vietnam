# Letter 018: notes

## Source

**This folder holds two letters.** The family labelled it "Letter 18_Petey".

1. **A letter to his little brother Petey** (scans 0089_b_Petey and 0089_a_Petey): one sheet of thin paper written on both sides in dark blue ink, in large, careful handwriting for a small boy. It is undated and signed "Your brother, Tommy", with a decorative flourish. It goes with the airmail envelope (0088) addressed in his hand to "Petey Jafuta" in West Allis, Wisconsin, with "Free" and the return address "Tom Jafuta, HHB 3/6 ARTY, APO SAN FRAN 96318". The envelope has no postmark and its back is blank.
2. **A letter to Dad, Mom & Kids** (scans 0090_b–0093_a): five pages of thin airmail paper in black ink, dated "today is July 9th" (the day is not quite certain) and written "back in Pleiku". It is signed "Your son & brother, Tom", with a decorative flourish and a postscript. Its envelope was not scanned.

The family letter's reading order is 0090_b, 0091_b (2), 0092_b (3, the number cut off at the top edge), 0093_b (4), 0093_a (last page). Each page runs on from the one before ("But a happier / one will be when this mess is over"; "I'm beat... / take good care").

### Reading edition: changes from the diplomatic text

- Each letter is given its own short heading. Line breaks are joined into paragraphs, following his indented paragraph starts. Page markers and the envelope appear in the diplomatic text only.
- "any- / ways" (split at a line end) is joined.
- His underline of "whole" is shown in italics. "BURP", which he wrote above "supper", is set in brackets after that word.
- Crossed-out words, flourishes and the long looping line near the end are left out. All of them are in the diplomatic text.
- His spelling, grammar and punctuation are unchanged, including "Your right", "Vellll??", "By for now", "Acoff", "guess this a part of life", "it don't look like it", "the VC NVA" (he wrote "VC" above "NVA"), "nite", "hay" and "HA".

## Open questions for the family

1. **Two letters, one folder.** The envelope is addressed to Petey and goes with the short letter to him. The family letter of July 9th must have come in its own envelope. **Decided (Andy, 2026-11-10):** present them in the book as two separate letters.
2. **When was the Petey letter written?** "Pretty soon you go up north"; "I'll miss your birthday Petey"; "Pretty soon I come home". So it was written before the family's summer trip up north and before Petey's birthday, probably in spring or early summer 1969. **Answered in part (Andy, 2026-11-10):** Petey's birthday is in August. So Dad wrote before Petey's August birthday and expected still to be away then (**interpretation**; Letter 012 gives his ETS as 21 August). Still open: was this the letter Petey "liked" (Letter 006), and is it the one Dad asks about on July 9th ("Did Petey ever get my letter?")?
3. ~~**Eddie Acoff.**~~ **Approved for publication** (Andy, 2026-11-10). Dad spoke of him often (see Family notes). Dad wrote "Acoff"; the Vietnam Veterans Memorial spells it "Acuff". Still open: would the family like to leave a remembrance on the Wall of Faces?
4. **The family.** "Give Dad, Mom, Kathy, Mary & Mike a big kiss & hug for me." This names his sisters Kathy and Mary and his brother Mike. Mike, also written "Mick" (Letters 009, 011 and 017), is his brother Michael (Andy, 2026-11-10). May all the siblings be named in the book?
5. **"Or no plane ride for you."** Was a plane ride for Petey a family plan or a joke between them?
6. **Petey's barber shop.** "Then we can go to your barber shop & buy a coke." Does anyone remember which barber shop?
7. **The vacation and the fish stories.** "How was the vacation... Especially the fish stories." Andy says "up north" usually meant West Bend. Was this vacation at West Bend, the "up north" of the Petey letter?
8. **The hippies.** "One thing the Hippies do that is right is their peace movement." It is his opinion in his own words. Publish as written?
9. **Readings to check:**

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "Well today is July 9th... I'm back in Pleiku now cause our operation in the Dak To - Ben Het area is over with for the time being anyways." This agrees with Letters 005 and 010, which have him at Dak To in late June 1969, and with Letter 001 (July 21st), which has him "back in Pleiku".
- **Interpretation:** The letter is very likely from **9 July 1969**.
- **Historical context:** The Vietnam Veterans Memorial lists Specialist Four Eddie Duane Acuff of San Antonio, Texas. He served with Headquarters and Headquarters Battery, 3rd Battalion, 6th Artillery, 52nd Artillery Group, I Field Force, and died on 23 June 1969. He is on the Wall at Panel 22W, Line 115. Records describe him as an artillery surveyor who died through hostile action, from multiple fragmentation wounds, in Kontum province. *Sources:* [The Virtual Wall, "Eddie Duane Acuff"](https://www.virtualwall.org/da/AcuffED01a.htm); [Vietnam Veterans Memorial Fund, Wall of Faces, Eddie D. Acuff](https://www.vvmf.org/Wall-of-Faces/175/EDDIE-D-ACUFF/page/2/); [Honor States, "Eddie Duane Acuff"](https://honorstates.org/index.php?id=259204).
- **Interpretation:** "Eddie Acoff" is very likely Eddie Duane Acuff. The first name matches, the surname is close, he served in Dad's battery (HHB 3/6 Artillery), and he died in Kontum province, where Dak To and Ben Het lie, two weeks before this letter. The letter does not say where or how the attack happened. Nothing here places Dad at it.
- **Historical context:** Ben Het was surrounded by North Vietnamese regiments from mid-June 1969, and the siege lifted in early July (see Letter 005 notes and sources). That fits "our operation in the Dak To - Ben Het area is over with for the time being".
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "I'll hate leaving all my friends behind... Just because I'll be home doesn't mean that the war (conflict?) will be over. For me - yes - but not for all those who remain here."
- **Unknown:** The exact date of the letter to Petey (before August, since he expected to miss Petey's August birthday), and Petey's age at the time (the family knows).
- **Cross-reference (from later letters):** About 28 June 1969 Dad wrote to Grandpa from Dak To, "A good friend of mine (Acoff) died along with others who got all messed up" (Letter 047).
