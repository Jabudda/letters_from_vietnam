# Letter 005: notes

## Source

Four sheets of lined notebook paper written in blue ink, with an air-mail envelope. Pages 2, 3 and 4 carry a number in a circle at the top. The letter opens in a darker pen; after two lines he writes "Just switched pens", and the rest of page 1, pages 2, 3 and 4 are in a lighter blue. The backs of pages 2 and 4 are in a darker blue again. The back of page 3 is the printed cover of a Stuart Hall "Writing Tablet, No. 1776", with no writing. There is no scan of the back of page 1. There is no earlier draft for this letter.

The envelope is addressed in his hand to the Jafuta Family in West Allis, Wisconsin. It has "Free" where a stamp would go, "VIA AIR MAIL" printed on it, and his return address "T. Jafuta, HHB 3/6 ARTY, APO SAN FRAN 96318". "6/19" is written on its face in a darker ink; the family believes Dad wrote it (Andy, 2026-11-10). The envelope is kept with this letter in the family's folder; that it is this letter's envelope is assumed, not proven.

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** He is at Dak To "right now", having arrived "yesterday by chopper"; he was "happy to leave Ben Het" and expects to "go back" in a few days.
- **Historical context:** In May–July 1969 North Vietnamese Army regiments besieged both Ben Het and Dak To. In mid-June Ben Het was completely surrounded; the siege was lifted in early July. *Sources:* [Time, "The Lesson of Ben Het"](https://time.com/archive/6637066/world-the-lesson-of-ben-het); [Stars and Stripes, 26 June 1969, via Southeast Asia Conflict](https://southeastasiaconflict.blogspot.com/2020/05/june-26-1969-ben-het-cidg-camp-is.html); [The VVA Veteran, the 1969 siege of Dak To](https://vvaveteran.org/32-1/32-1_dakto69.html).
- **Historical context:** A platoon of Battery A, 3rd Battalion, 6th Artillery (Dad's battalion) was at Fire Support Base 12, Ben Het, from May to July 1969. On 9 June the A Battery platoon there had six men wounded by recoilless-rifle fire. *Sources:* [1st Battalion, 92nd Artillery, Operational Report, May–Jul 1969](https://www.bravecannons.org/History/ORLL/j_may69-jul69.html); [1/92nd Field Artillery Association, "Ben Het/Dak To, May–July 1969"](https://www.bravecannons.org/hist_benhet.html).
- **Historical context:** Reporters flocked to the siege. *Time* wrote that, hard-pressed for stories in a quieter war, the press corps in Vietnam "seized eagerly on Ben Het". *Source:* [Time, "The Lesson of Ben Het"](https://time.com/archive/6637066/world-the-lesson-of-ben-het).
- **Unknown:** Whether the CBS film he mentions was broadcast, and whether he can be seen in it. CBS Evening News broadcasts from 1969 are indexed by the [Vanderbilt Television News Archive](https://tvnews.library.vanderbilt.edu/), which lends copies; this is a research lead.
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "I guess their going to name our hill Fire Base Hawkins cause he was the first to die when we took the hill."
- **Historical context:** Corporal Harold Fredrick Hawkins, of Coldwater, Michigan, served in Battery A, 3rd Battalion, 6th Artillery. He was wounded on 13 June 1969 and died on 18 June 1969, aged 22. His name is on the Vietnam Veterans Memorial, Panel W22, Line 77, and on the Vietnam War memorial in Coldwater. *Sources:* [The Virtual Wall, Harold Fredrick Hawkins](https://virtualwall.org/dh/HawkinsHF01a.htm); [The Historical Marker Database, Vietnam War KIA, Coldwater, Michigan](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=165455).
- **Historical context:** He was born on 4 January 1947. The Virtual Wall gives his full unit as Battery A, 3rd Battalion, 6th Artillery, 52nd Artillery Group, I Field Force. The Coldwater memorial stands in Oak Grove Cemetery and lists him as an Army corporal (E-4). *Sources:* as above.
- **Research lead:** The Vietnam Veterans Memorial Fund's [Wall of Faces](https://www.vvmf.org/Wall-of-Faces/) has a page for every name on The Wall, with a photo and remembrances left by family and friends. His page can be found by searching "Harold F Hawkins". The family could leave a remembrance there linking his name to Dad's letter.
- **Interpretation:** Same battalion, same battery as the guns at Ben Het, and the same days: he is very likely the Hawkins Dad meant. Whether a firebase was ever named for him is **unknown**; no record of a "Fire Base Hawkins" has been found.
- **Historical context:** Special Forces strike forces in the Central Highlands (the "Mike Force") included Khmer Krom, ethnic Cambodians from South Vietnam, alongside Montagnards. *Source:* [MIKE Force (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIKE_Force).
- **Interpretation:** The "Cambodians" Dad met were very likely such troops. His explanation, that "Cambodia doesn't want the NVA on their border", is his own understanding at the time.
- **Historical context:** Ben Het was a Special Forces camp held by a 12-man US Special Forces team (Detachment A-244) and Montagnard tribesmen of the Civilian Irregular Defense Group (CIDG), who lived in the camp **with their families**, alongside South Vietnamese (ARVN) troops and US artillery. In early 1969 there were about 440 CIDG, 511 ARVN soldiers, 207 artillerymen and 25 US advisers there. *Sources:* [Stars and Stripes, 26 June 1969, via Southeast Asia Conflict](https://southeastasiaconflict.blogspot.com/2020/05/june-26-1969-ben-het-cidg-camp-is.html); [Ben Het Camp (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Het_Camp).
- **Historical context:** "Montagnard" is French for "mountain people", the name for the Central Highlands' indigenous tribes. From 1962 on, US Special Forces worked with them more and more in the highlands, and the Montagnard CIDG soldiers were known as "strikers". *Sources:* [ARSOF History, Veritas vol. 5, no. 4, on the CIDG program](https://arsof-history.org/articles/pdf/v5n4_cidg.pdf); [ARSOF History, "The Montagnard Uprising of September 1964"](https://arsof-history.org/articles/v3n2_montagnard_page_1.html).
- **Interpretation:** The family remembers Dad describing the Montagnards as warriors who brought their whole families to fight. That matches what the sources say of Ben Het, where the CIDG strikers' families lived inside the camp.
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** They had "been getting hit with gas lately"; he calls it tear gas, "not harmful but sure the heck irritating".
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "about 1,440 hours to go until THE DAY", which is 60 days, and "next month I'll be home". He expected to leave Vietnam around August 1969. Dad served two tours in Vietnam (family); this letter looks ahead to going home.
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "Dad knows what I mean cause he was in the same 'boat' many years ago." His father, Peter F. Jafuta, served in the US Army in World War II (VA record; see Letter 004).
- **Historical context (NARA, 2026-11-10):** Battery A, 3d Battalion, 6th Artillery was at Ben Het (Fire Support Base 12, the North Hill) from 1 May 1969, and from 10 June was under the 6th Battalion, 14th Artillery. See `research/ben-het-1969-records.md`.
