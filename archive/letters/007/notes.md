# Letter 007: notes

## Source

Two sheets of lined notebook paper written in black ink, four pages, each numbered in a circle at the top right (1 to 4). Scans 0035_a1 and 0035_a2 are pages 1 and 2; 0036 and 0037 are pages 3 and 4. A fold runs across each page. Signed "Your ol' son, Tommy".

The envelope is addressed in his hand to "Mrs. Peter Jafuta" (very likely his mother, addressed by her husband's name in the style of the time; interpretation) in West Allis, Wisconsin, with "Free" where a stamp would go and his return address "T. Jafuta, HHB 3/6 ARTY, APO San Francisco 96318". There is no postmark. Only its front was scanned. The letter is to Mom, so the envelope and letter match. It ends "guess Ill close for now and write Dad a letter too"; that letter to Dad would have gone separately.

## Open questions for the family

1. **This is a second-tour letter.** He writes "I never did want to let you all know I was coming back over here but of course you found out", of a letter Mom sent him "down in Oakland", and of a phone call home "before I came here". Does the family know when his second tour began?
2. **The date.** "Sunday, the 14th", and "It sure seems funny that Im 21 now... Somebody asked me how old I was today". Andy has given Dad's birthday (early January; the exact date is kept private, see Letter 002 notes), so he turned 21 in **January 1969**. This letter is very likely from **mid-January 1969**, about a week after his 21st birthday, during his second tour (interpretation). One thing does not fit: 14 January 1969 was a Tuesday, not a Sunday (12 January was the Sunday). Andy says Dad often mixed up dates, sometimes swapping his own birthday and Grandpa's (13 January). Is "about 14 January 1969" good enough for the book?
3. ~~**"Cil".**~~ Answered: Cil was Dad's aunt (Andy, 2026-11-10). See Family notes.
4. **"What happened to me when I was back home."** He writes that his "mind just snapped for awhile" and that "all that tension just 'exploded' in me while I was at home". Does the family know what happened? This is the most personal passage in the letters so far; see Privacy below.
5. **"An education place in Pleiku."** He had a "lead" on how to contact one. Did he take courses during his second tour? (Letter 006 mentions "blue-printing in one of my courses".)
6. **The letter to Dad.** He closes by saying he will write Dad a letter too. Does the family have that letter? (The envelope reads "Mrs. Peter Jafuta"; please confirm that is how Grandma was addressed.)
7. **Readings to check:** "so mixed up I" (page 1); "you an Cil" (page 2); "accept it a loose faith" (perhaps "or").

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** He is back in Vietnam ("I never did want to let you all know I was coming back over here") and in reach of Pleiku ("an education place in Pleiku somewhere"). The return address is still HHB 3/6 ARTY, APO 96318, as on Letters 001–006.
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** Before leaving he was "down in Oakland", where a letter from Mom reached him, and he phoned home "before I came here".
- **Historical context:** During the Vietnam War, the Oakland Army Base in California was a major transit station for soldiers on their way to and from Vietnam, and the largest military port complex in the world. *Sources:* [Oakland Army Base (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_Army_Base); [California State Military Museum, "Oakland Army Base"](https://militarymuseum.org/OaklandArmyBase.html).
- **Interpretation:** "Down in Oakland" very likely means the Oakland Army Base, where he would have been processed before flying back to Vietnam.
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "Ive seen death many times but I just can't believe it will ever happen to our family."
- **Interpretation:** In his own words, he describes holding feelings back until they "explode", a time at home when his "mind just snapped for awhile", feeling he might "crack up", and not seeking "help or advice from others". The family has written that Dad's generation of veterans were the pioneers of what came to be called PTSD (Letter 004 notes). This letter does not name any condition, and none is assumed here; it is Dad describing his own experience.
