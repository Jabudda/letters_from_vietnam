# Letter 016: notes

## Source

Three sheets torn from a spiral notebook (red rules, a margin line, torn holes along the top), written in grey (pencil or a soft pen), six pages. Pages 2 to 6 are numbered at the top right. Addressed "Dear Family", dated in the text "Today is March 4th or 5th", in answer to a family letter "dated Feb. 25th". Signed "Your son & brother, Tom", with a decorative flourish below, and a P.S.

The airmail envelope (red and blue border, printed "VIA AIR MAIL / CORREO AEREO / PAR AVION" label) is addressed in his hand to the "Jafuta Family" in West Allis, Wisconsin, with "Free" and the return address "T. Jafuta, HHB 3/6 ARTY, APO SAN FRANCISCO 96318". No postmark. The back has blue ballpoint scribbles that are not words, perhaps a child's.

## Open questions for the family

1. **The year.** "Today is March 4th or 5th", second tour ("But here we are again"). He agrees he wishes "they would start bombing the north", which fits the months after the US stopped bombing North Vietnam on 1 November 1968. So this is very likely **March 1969** (interpretation), between Letter 002 (22 January 1969) and Letter 003 (11 May 1969). Confirm?
2. **"This time last month."** "That's when I was getting 'short'. This time last month I only had a few days left in Viet Nam and how happy I was!! But here we are again." Read literally, he left Vietnam a month earlier; read with the rest, he may mean this time last **year**, at the end of his first tour (early 1968). The words stay as written. Does the family know when his first tour ended?
3. **Mom's birthday.** "Ma, I'm sorry I missed your birthday." Late February or early March? (Only if the family wants it in the book.)
4. **"Our 'neighbor' got back from Korea."** Who was the neighbor?
5. **The girl who called for his address.** "I really was surprised Jackie [reading uncertain] called to get my address... Her mother just better not be playing 'cupid'." Andy checked off the reading "Jackie" in the review checklist. The letter says she called for his address and that he had thought "she was married already"; separately he writes, "I got to darn close to getting married once already. Sure am glad I didn't." **Decided:** publish the "getting married" line as written. Still open: may Jackie be named in the book, or only her first name as he wrote it?
6. **Pat and Harley-Davidson.** "I hope Pat gets that job. Harley Davidson is a good company to work for." Is this the same Pat as Letter 009 (whose "military obligation is over with")? Did he get the job?
7. **Chuckie.** "I sure hope Chuckie makes out OK! I just don't know if that guy will ever learn... I just hope it doesn't cost him or others their lives." **Answered (Andy, 2026-11-10):** Chuckie was Grandma's brother (Dad's uncle). Grandma's obituary names her brother Charles Collins, so Chuckie is very likely Charles Collins (interpretation; please confirm). **Decided:** publish as written. Still open, only if the family wants it: was Chuckie in the service, or was Dad worried about something at home?
8. **Snoopy.** "(Snoopy just walked in)" puts Snoopy in Vietnam with him (see Letters 002, 006, 008). A camp dog?
9. **The NVA and Catholics.** The family wrote that "the NVA didn't draft Catholics"; Dad disagreed. Does anyone remember where that idea came from?
10. **Readings to check:** "we've been really busy late" (page 1); "Jackie" and "Was called" (page 2); "summer school for driving and math"; "to easy of a going guy"; the crossed-out words on page 4; "had guard last nigh".

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** He is back in Vietnam for a second tour ("But here we are again"), with HHB 3/6 ARTY, APO 96318. He "had guard last nigh" and would have "the 'honors' again tomorrow".
- **Historical context:** On 31 October 1968 President Johnson announced that all bombing of North Vietnam would stop, effective 1 November 1968, ending Operation Rolling Thunder. US bombing of North Vietnam then largely stayed halted until 1972. *Sources:* [Office of the Historian, US Department of State, FRUS 1964–68 vol. VII, doc. 169](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1964-68v07/d169); [HISTORY.com, "Operation Rolling Thunder"](https://www.history.com/topics/vietnam-war/operation-rolling-thunder); [Vietnam Air Losses, "The End of Rolling Thunder (Nov 1968)"](https://vietnamairlosses.com/index.php/sidelines/1968/nov68).
- **Interpretation:** "I too wish they would start bombing the north as you said" fits a letter written after the November 1968 halt.
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** His view of North Vietnamese conscription: "They draft anybody they can get, - men, women and children... Its forced upon them. I and many others would do the same if they gave me a choice of my family or me." This is his opinion as written; it is not checked here as a historical claim.
- **Historical context:** "Short" (getting short, a short-timer) was soldiers' slang for having little time left in Vietnam. *Source:* [University of Virginia, "Sixties Project" glossary of Vietnam War terms (Q–T)](https://www2.iath.virginia.edu/sixties/HTML_docs/Resources/Glossary/Sixties_Term_Gloss_Q_T.html).
- **Historical context:** "OD" is olive drab, the Army's green. (General usage; his own gloss, "(army green)", is in the letter.)
- **Unknown:** Where he was when he wrote.
