# Letter 021: notes

## Source

Three sheets of lined notebook paper (blue rules), written in black ink on one side, five pages. The pages are not numbered; each runs on from the one before ("then his / parents"; "at an early / age"; "in a nutshell / hay Mom?"; "I sure am proud / of every one of you"). Addressed "Dear Mom" and dated in the text "Today is May 20 or so". Signed "Your ol' son, Tom", with a decorative flourish below.

The envelope is printed "AFTER 5 DAYS RETURN TO … ZIP CODE" and is torn at the top right. Dad addressed it to **"June Jafuta"** (Grandma by her own name; Letter 007 was addressed to "Mrs. Peter Jafuta") in West Allis, Wisconsin, with the return address "T. Jafuta, HHB 3/6 Arty, APO SAN FRAN 96318". There is no stamp and no postmark visible. In the torn top right corner, in another hand, is "7/" followed by a torn-off mark (perhaps a date written on arrival, like the "6/24" on Letter 010; interpretation). Only the front was scanned.

## Open questions for the family

1. **The year.** "Today is May 20 or so". He is near the end of a tour ("soon we'll all be together again"; "No more army for me"; "I remember the last time I was home"), so this is a second-tour letter. Letters 009 (May 5) and 008 (May 10) are from May 1969, so this is very likely **about 20 May 1969** (interpretation). Confirm?
2. **The envelope.** Addressed to "June Jafuta", Grandma by her own first name. Who wrote "7/…" in the torn corner, and could it be a date the letter arrived (July would be late for a letter of 20 May)? The rest is torn away.
3. **Dad's letter.** "Dad hit the nail on the head when he wrote and told me 'that I may think I was "cheated" out of my [possible reading: early] manhood' by being over here so long." Does Grandpa's letter survive?
4. **"Happiness is…"** Dad copied a passage he had just read, "by Ralph Emerson". The passage is not by Emerson (see Historical notes). Did the family keep a clipping or card with it? Did it mean something to Grandma later?
5. **Mischief.** "I always had a problem of getting into mischief." Any family stories the family would like in the chapter?
6. **Readings to check:**

   - "[possible reading: early]" written above a crossed-out word (page 2);
   - "[possible reading: Want] to hear it Mom" (page 3);

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "I've cried many times over here when someone I knew got hurt bad or died."
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "All the time I think about when I'll be a civilian some day"; "No more army for me."
- **Historical context:** The passage Dad copied ("to laugh often and much; to win the respect of intelligent people… This is success") was widely printed as Ralph Waldo Emerson's, but it is not found in Emerson's work. It comes from Bessie Anderson Stanley's prize-winning 1905 answer to "What is success?", reworded and credited to Emerson by a newspaper columnist, Albert E. Wiggam, in 1951, after which the Emerson attribution spread. *Sources:* [Freakonomics, "Quotes Uncovered: Did Emerson Define Success?" (Fred Shapiro, Yale Book of Quotations), 2009](https://freakonomics.com/2009/03/quotes-uncovered-did-emerson-define-success); [Wikipedia, "Bessie Anderson Stanley"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessie_Anderson_Stanley); [WIST, "Ralph Waldo Emerson (misattributed)"](https://wist.info/emerson-ralph-waldo/46825/).
- **Interpretation:** Dad's wording matches the later, Emerson-credited version, so he very likely read it in a newspaper, magazine or card that gave Emerson's name.
- **Unknown:** Where he was when he wrote.
