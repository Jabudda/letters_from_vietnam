# Letter 029: notes

## Source

Four sheets of printed USO letterhead ("USO / A Home Away from Home", with the member agencies listed at the foot), written in black ink, eight pages. The first two sheets are written on one side only, the last two on both sides. Pages 2, 3 and 4 are numbered in circles; page 5 ends with "OVER". Addressed "Dear Dad, Mom & Kids" and dated in the text "Today is Easter". Signed "Love & miss all of you, Tom", with a decorative flourish, and two postscripts signed "Love, Tom".

At the top of page 1, in pencil and in another hand (block capitals), someone wrote **"EASTER" (underlined) and "APRIL 6, 1969"**. No envelope was scanned. The master folder is labelled "Letter 29_USO_Letterhead".

## Open questions for the family

1. **The date (confirmed by the calendar).** "Today is Easter." Someone in the family wrote "EASTER APRIL 6, 1969" at the top. Easter Sunday 1969 fell on 6 April, which agrees with the rest of the letter (the school early out in 1969, "the month after next" being June). **Verified:** Sunday 6 April 1969. Who wrote the pencil date?
2. **The R&R to Australia that didn't happen.** "We've been down here since the 31st and haven't seen Australia lately. We finally gave up yesterday." He and John waited at Cam Ranh Bay from 31 March for an R&R flight to Australia, gave up on 5 April, and were flying back to the unit on Easter Sunday. **This explains Letter 028** (8 April 1969): "We got back to the hill yesterday… we never left country yet. You should have gotten my last letter & pictures by now. I mailed them down in Cam Rahn Bay." This is very likely that "last letter" (interpretation).
3. **The pictures.** "We even went swimming down by the ocean almost every day. I'll send some pictures of that to you all." Do any photos from Cam Ranh Bay survive?
4. **John.** "We (John & I) sure got some nice peaceful sleep." The same John who was in the hospital in May (Letters 009 and 003), whom Dad visited in Phan Thiet in July (Letter 023), and who "left" in June (Letter 027)? (Interpretation.)
5. ~~**"Sure hope ol' Snoopy is OK."**~~ **Answered (Andy, 2026-11-10):** "Dad's Vietnam dog was Snoopy, but he may have been KIA." Dad's writing of the name looks like "Snappy" here; the family's reading is used. Earlier note kept for the record: "The hill got hit again as we were told. Sure hope ol' [possible reading: Snappy] is OK, & everyone else of course." This looks like a dog left on "the hill". In Letter 025 (June) Dad wrote of a dog "to replace Fritz & Snappy", and in Letter 027 the word read as "Snoopy" ("Snoopy just came in all full of mud") may be written the same way. **Was the dog Dad had in Vietnam called Snoopy or Snappy, or were there two dogs?** (Andy has said Snoopy was Dad's dog in Vietnam, the dog on the left in photo P002.)
6. **"Last year at this time I was home on leave from over here."** So around Easter 1968 Dad was home on leave in the middle of his time in Vietnam. **Andy (2026-11-10): "Easter 1968 sounds right."** (See Letter 022, "When I came home last time and seen all your faces", and Letter 016's "this time last month".)
7. **Brunner, Kruse, Wolf and Del.** "About the same time as when Brunner Kruse & Wolf got killed. And before that it was Del and many others - especially during Tet of last year." See Historical notes: two of these names match soldiers of Dad's own battery who died on 29 March 1968. **Approved for publication (Andy, 2026-11-10: "2. Approved").** Did Dad ever talk about them?
8. **Money.** "I'll be sending this money home… Did you get the last $200 yet? From March's check." (Letter 008 also asks about an April $200 check.) Fine to publish?
9. **The school early-out letter.** "It has to be phrased about in the same words as the copy I sent you." Does that copy, or the college's letter, survive?
10. **Readings to check:** "cleanist"; "Brunner" (could be read "Brummer"); "Del"; the pencil note at the top.

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (calendar):** Easter Sunday in 1969 was 6 April.
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** From 31 March to 6 April 1969 he was at Cam Ranh Bay, waiting with John for an R&R flight to Australia that did not come; he swam in the ocean, slept, and wrote on USO letterhead. He expected to fly back to his unit that day.
- **Historical context:** Australia (Sydney) was one of the out-of-country R&R destinations for U.S. servicemen in Vietnam, and Cam Ranh Bay was one of the R&R departure points in Vietnam. *Sources:* [Australian Government, Department of Veterans' Affairs, "Rest and recreation in Sydney during Vietnam War"](https://anzacportal.dva.gov.au/wars-and-missions/vietnam-war/home/rest-and-recreation-in-sydney); [Pan Am Historical Foundation, "Pan Am and Vietnam"](https://www.panam.org/jet-age/pan-am-vietnam).
- **Historical context:** The Virtual Wall lists **Cpl Hans Wolfgang Brunner** of Oswego, Illinois, and **Sgt Kendal Robert Kruse** of Glenwood, Iowa, both of HHB, 3rd Battalion, 6th Artillery (Dad's battery), 52nd Artillery Group, I Field Force, both died on **29 March 1968**. *Sources:* [The Virtual Wall, "Hans Wolfgang Brunner"](https://www.virtualwall.org/db/BrunnerHW01a.htm); [The Virtual Wall, "Kendal Robert Kruse"](https://www.virtualwall.org/dk/KruseKR01a.htm).
- **Interpretation:** "Brunner" and "Kruse" in the letter are very likely these two men: same battery, and 29 March 1968 is about a week before Easter 1968 (14 April), when Dad says he was home on leave. **Unknown:** "Wolf" (no soldier of that name in HHB 3/6 Artillery was found in a first search) and "Del".
- **Historical context:** The Tet Offensive began at the end of January 1968 ("Tet of last year"). (General knowledge.)
- **Unknown:** Where "the hill" was.
- **Cross-reference (from a later-numbered letter):** On 25 March 1969, at Pleiku, Dad wrote "In a few more days I hope to be in Australia but that all depends on if I get my money orders in time" (Letter 036). This letter is how that plan turned out.
