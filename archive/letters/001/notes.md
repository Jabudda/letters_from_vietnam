### Reading edition: changes from the diplomatic text

- Line breaks joined into paragraphs, following his indented paragraph starts. Page markers and the small star drawn above "21" are kept in the diplomatic text only.
- Circled numbers ①②③ printed as (1)(2)(3).
- `future[.]`: he ends page 4 with no full stop and starts page 5 with a new sentence; the bracketed stop marks this.
- `on[e]`: he wrote "last on on"; the bracket supplies the missing letter.
- His spelling and contractions are unchanged: Im, Ive, thats, its, hay, nite, "under strengthed".

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** He writes that he is "back in Pleiku with my unit now", and is counting down to leaving the unit ("about 27 or so guards left").
- **Verified fact:** Apollo 11's lunar module landed on the Moon at 20:17 UTC on 20 July 1969, and the crew splashed down in the Pacific on 24 July 1969. *Sources:* Wikipedia, "Apollo 11" (link supplied by Andy); The Planetary Society, "Apollo 11" mission timeline; Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Apollo 11".
- **Historical context:** Armstrong's moonwalk was broadcast live to an estimated 600 million viewers, about one-fifth of the world's population. *Source:* Wikipedia, "Apollo 11".
- **Interpretation:** He writes "I hear they landed on the moon", asks the family "Did you see it on TV" and hopes for "a re-run… when I get home". That suggests he heard the news but did not see the broadcast himself.
- **Interpretation:** The letter is dated "July 21st", speaks of the moon landing as just happened, and hopes the astronauts "got back safely… We'll find out pretty soon". That fits **21 July 1969**, before the splashdown. The year is not written on the letter; the date was confirmed by Andy on approval (2026-11-10).
- **Interpretation:** He asks for the family's last letter "on or about Aug 10-12", so he expected to leave in mid to late August. Letter 047 counts down to "Aug. 21st"; the two may refer to the same departure, but that needs confirming.
- **Interpretation:** "Under strength" is a military term for a unit with fewer people than it is authorized. If that is his meaning, the unit was short of men, which would explain his expecting guard duty "every nite".
- **Historical context:** A 37-day strike hit the Schlitz, Pabst and Miller breweries in Milwaukee in 1969. *Source:* United Press International, "Workers strike at Schlitz brewery", 1 June 1981 (found by Andy, 2026-11-10). The article gives the length but not the months.
- **Historical context:** The 1969 strike was under way in mid-June: a Milwaukee Public Library photograph dated 17 June 1969 shows Brewery Workers Local 9 pickets at the Schlitz brewery, and its description notes that Miller and Pabst were also on strike and that the strike lasted five weeks. *Source:* Milwaukee Public Library Digital Collections, "Police observe Schlitz brewery strikers". A brewing-history article also places it in a hot summer (giving 36 days rather than 37). *Source:* Good Beer Hunting, "More Than Just Free Beer", 2021.
- **Interpretation:** A five-week strike already under way on 17 June would have ended around mid to late July 1969. That fits his writing on July 21st that he is "glad to hear that the beer strike is finally off", and supports dating the letter to 21 July 1969. The exact end date is still to be found (the *Milwaukee Journal* or *Milwaukee Sentinel* for July 1969 would have it).
- **Note on the newspaper image found 2026-11-10:** The *Milwaukee Journal* front page Andy found is dated Monday, February 10, 1969. Its lead headline, "Agreement Reached at Technical College", appears to concern a different dispute, probably the Milwaukee Area Technical College faculty strike that winter (see Wisconsin Examiner, "Milwaukee's 1969 strike offers lessons…", 10 Feb 2021), not the brewery strike. Kept as a research lead only. It is a newspaper page under copyright, so it isn't for publication without permission.
