# Letter 020: notes

## Source

Three sheets of lined paper with blue rules, written on both sides in blue-black ink. There are six pages; the third and fifth are numbered "2" and "3" in a circle. Each sheet's back carries on from its front. The reading order is 0103_b, 0103_a, 0104_b, 0104_a, 0105_b, 0105_a, and each page runs on from the one before ("We got alot of years / of hunting to do"; "knowing / what you are going through"; "The / other one"; "Also … OK? / Is he still going"; "a day closer / to getting home").

The letter opens "Hi gang," and is headed "Oct. 2[7?], 196[?] / Viet Nam"; the last digit of the year runs off the edge of the scan. The text says "Today is Oct. 29". It is signed "Love all of you, Tommy", with a P.S.

The envelope is addressed in his hand to the "Jafuta Family" in West Allis, Wisconsin. It has "Free" where a stamp would go and the return address "T. Jafuta, HHB [the second letter looks like an A] 3/6 ARTY, APO SAN FRANCISCO 96318". **It is postmarked "ARMY & AIR FORCE POSTAL SERVICE, OCT 30, 1968, APO 96318."** This is the first envelope in the collection so far with a readable postmark. Only the front was scanned.

## Open questions for the family

1. **The year (now fixed by the postmark).** The envelope is postmarked **30 October 1968** at APO 96318. The letter was therefore written on 29 October 1968 (his "Today is Oct. 29"). The heading's year digit is cut off at the scan edge; it would be "1968". This is the earliest dated letter so far. It shows that by October 1968 he was already back in Vietnam on his second tour. Does that match what the family knows?
2. **Jim coming "here".** "I kinda expect to see ol' Jim [reading uncertain] coming here in the next couple of days… by the 31st or 1st." Is this Jim Theilke, arriving for his own second tour? (See Letters 006, 008 and 011: "He too thinks we made a mistake coming back.")
3. **"So ol' Mike is in J.A."** Is J.A. Junior Achievement? Dad says he and Pat were in it too.
4. **Sharyn, Karen, Davey, Tim, Beverly, Pat, Lee, Eleanor.** "Sharyn won't have her Baby until December… her husband is over here somewhere"; "Karen did have a small accident"; "that picture of me standing with Karen"; "send Lee's" (plumbing book); "Davey's address"; "Tim… still going with Beverly"; "So Pat's getting out"; "Eleanor's address (your mom's & sister's)". Who are they, and may they be named? **Lee:** the family had an Uncle Lee, a Navy veteran of World War II (Andy, 2026-11-10). Is he the Lee of this letter? **Tim:** the family has a cousin Tim Collins (Andy, 2026-11-10). Is he the Tim who was "still going with Beverly"? Is Eleanor Grandma's mother's name?
5. **The plumbing book.** "I also got the plumbing book… When I finish this plumbing book I have I'll tell you and sure would appreciate it if you would send Lee's." Dad was studying plumbing in Vietnam in 1968. Grandpa owned and ran Pete's Plumbing Service (Andy). Was "Lee's" book Uncle Lee's (**interpretation**, not confirmed)? Did he work in plumbing too?
6. ~~**Grandpa's back.**~~ Context from Andy: Grandpa owned and ran Pete's Plumbing Service, so the "heavy stuff" was very likely plumbing work (interpretation).
7. **Petey's guitar and singing lessons.** Did Petey keep it up?
8. **The bird.** "I shot a big ol' bird the other day and gave it to a villager." Publish as written?
9. **Readings to check:**
   - the heading date and year;
   - "HHB" on the envelope (written like "HAB");
   - "Jim" (page 1);
   - "Also Tim is in OK?" (back of page 2);
   - "Beverly";

   - the crossed-out word before "out" (page 3).

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (postmark):** Mailed through the Army & Air Force Postal Service at APO 96318 on 30 October 1968.
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "I'm sorry but its for a cause which I believe in"; "I'll feel good too, knowing that I fulfilled my military obligation in the best way I could. Sure at times I'm scared over here and feel like crying, but I guess that's natural - as you said."
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "I never want to have to see my brothers come over here or to some other war."
- **Historical context:** On 31 October 1968, the day after this letter was postmarked, President Johnson announced that all bombing of North Vietnam would stop from 1 November (see Letter 016 notes and sources). The letter, written two days earlier, does not mention it.
- **Unknown:** Where he was when he wrote.
