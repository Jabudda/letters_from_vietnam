# Letter 037: notes

## Source

Two sheets of faintly ruled paper written in blue ink on one side, four pages. Page 2 is numbered in a circle; the others are not. Each page runs on from the one before ("Thats one / thing that a guy will never get over"; "And not someone real / special"). Addressed "Dear Dad, Mom & Kids" and dated in the text "today is May 17th". Signed "Your son & brother, Tom", with a flourish; most of the last page is blank.

The airmail envelope (red and blue striped border, printed "VIA AIR MAIL") is addressed in his hand to the "Jafuta Family" in West Allis, Wisconsin, with "Free" and the return address "Tom Jafuta, HHB 3/6 ARTY, APO SAN FRAN 96318". There is no postmark. Only the front was scanned.

## Open questions for the family

1. **The date.** "today is May 17th", "still out at the Oasis". Letters 002 and 003 (10–11 May 1969) are from the Oasis, so this is very likely **17 May 1969** (interpretation, a strong one).
2. **Steve [possible reading: Dott] (an answer to Letter 003).** On 11 May 1969 Dad wrote "Steve [possible reading: Dott] may be worse off I fear… Steve's unit didn't know where he was" (Letter 003). Six days later, at the Oasis, he "saw Steve [possible reading: Dott] again" on a morning patrol: Steve was all right then. The surname is written the same way in both letters. What was his surname? Andy (2026-11-10): "I think Steve Dott was from Tennessee and a good looking guy. Dad mentioned someone going home to Tennessee and they excitedly asked him how his leave was, expecting tales of chasing women, but instead, he went hunting and fishing while in Tennessee." If so, Steve [Dott] was an army friend from Tennessee, not the hometown "Stevie" of Letter 019 (the turkey farm) (interpretation). Is he also the Steve of "Steve's folks" in Letter 002?
3. **"Steve is really a gung-ho guy… I was like that my first time here for a few months but finally got to thinking."** A rare line about how his own view changed between his first and second tours.
4. **St. Christopher.** "I heard someone talking that the Church has rejected alot of 'saints' such as St. Cristopher… Many times I prayed to St. Cris." Did Grandma write back about it? Did Dad carry a St. Christopher medal?
5. **"A good ol' fish fry hay, Mom? Or better yet - pheasant or rabbit."** Family meals he was looking forward to.
6. **Readings to check:** "[possible reading: Dott]" (the same shape as in Letter 003); the crossed-out word before "see Steve".

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** On 17 May (1969, interpretation) he was "still out at the Oasis" and might leave the next day; he had guard the night before, went on "a dumb morning patrol", and got "soaked" in the rain.
- **Historical context:** Landing Zone Oasis, about 24 km southwest of Pleiku, was attacked on 10–11 May 1969 (see Letters 002 and 003 and the map). *Source:* [Wikipedia, "Landing Zone Oasis"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_Zone_Oasis).
- **Historical context:** On Friday 9 May 1969 the Vatican announced a revision of the Roman Catholic calendar that dropped about 200 saints' feast days from the universal calendar, St. Christopher's among them; his memorial was left to local calendars. The news caused such alarm that on 11 May the Vatican newspaper said press reports had given rise to "unfounded alarm". Dad heard about it at the Oasis about a week later. *Sources:* [Wikipedia, "General Roman Calendar of 1969"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_1969) (citing *The New York Times*, 10 May 1969); [*The Press* (Christchurch), "Rome Explains Calendar Reform", Reuter, 12 May 1969](https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/newspapers/CHP19690512.2.106); [The Tablet, "St. Christopher Still Guides Travelers"](https://thetablet.org/?p=280411).
- **Historical context (NARA, 2026-11-10):** The battalion's 1968 award recommendation records that 3/6 Arty ran a forward tactical command post at LZ Oasis, west of Pleiku. See `research/ben-het-1969-records.md` (source 2).
