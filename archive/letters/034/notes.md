# Letter 034: notes

## Source

Four sheets of thin airmail paper written in blue ink on one side, seven pages. Only page 4 is numbered (in a circle); the others are not. Each page runs on from the one before ("Boy it sure is hot & / humid out today"; "it floods [possible reading: easy] / which we found out the / hard way"; "guess I'll close / for now"; "Thats what you / once said hay Mom?"). Addressed "Dear Dad, Mom & Kids" and dated in the text "Today is June 18th". Signed "Your son & brother, Tom", with a flourish. Most of the last page is blank, with a small tear and stains. At the top left of page 3 are three small figures, perhaps in another hand (possible reading: 31 / 30 / 3; meaning unknown).

The envelope is the printed "UNITED STATES ARMED FORCES - VIETNAM" kind with a map of Vietnam and a red and blue airmail stripe. It is addressed in his hand to the "Jafuta Family" in West Allis, Wisconsin, with the return address "T. Jafuta, HHB 3/6 ARTY, APO SAN FRAN 96318". There is no postmark. **"6/18" is written in another hand**, like the dates on the envelopes of Letters 010, 012, 023 and 025. Only the front was scanned.

## Open questions for the family

1. **The date.** "Today is June 18th", "Today is [possible reading: 63] days left in the army at the mostest. … Actually 62 days left". 18 June plus 63 days is 20 August, which fits his ETS of 21 August (Letter 026). So this is **18 June 1969** (interpretation, a strong one). "6/18" on the envelope agrees.
2. **"We're still up in Ben Het yet."** This is Dad, in his own words, at Ben Het on 18 June 1969, during the siege. Andy (2026-11-10): "I can confirm he was."
3. **Deacon.** "Deacon and I are living in this old French bunker down near the perimeter." In Letter 026 (7 June) Dad, about to go "up around Dak To again tomorrow near Ben Het to help our 'A' baty out", wrote "At least we'll get to see Deacon out there." Who was Deacon? Does anyone know his real name?
4. **"The word is … we're suppose to move to Dak To for a couple week and then back to Pleiku."** Letter 005 (19 or 20 June 1969) has him "up here in Dak To", and Letter 025 (about 21 June) speaks of the men "that came from Ben Het". So the move very likely happened within a day or two (interpretation).
5. **Vacation in "sunny Wisconsin".** Where did the family go on vacation in summer 1969?
6. **"Thats what you once said hay Mom?"** Grandma's saying that "time has a way of passing". Does the family remember it?
7. **Readings to check:** "[possible reading: won't it]" (page 2); "[possible reading: 63]" (page 3); "[possible reading: easy]" (page 4); the small figures at the top of page 3; the mark at the top right of the envelope.

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** On 18 June (1969, interpretation) he wrote "We're still up in Ben Het yet", that they were "hold up" with "not much to do except for things that keep us operational. And that we have to do at night time", that water was "rationed a helmet full a day", and that he and Deacon had moved into "this old French bunker down near the perimeter" because it looked "much safer then our own".
- **Historical context:** Ben Het was surrounded by North Vietnamese regiments in June 1969 and shelled daily; the road from Dak To was cut, and supplies came in by air. An AP report from Ben Het printed on 27 June 1969 said the American artillerymen there had been out of fresh water for five days; *Time* (11 July 1969) also wrote that the defenders lacked fresh water and hot food. *Sources:* AP, "Men At Ben Het Feel Victimized", *Daily Intelligencer Journal*, Lancaster, PA, 27 June 1969 (clipping on the [614arty.org "Siege of Ben Het" page](https://614arty.org/pages/ben_hetpg.html)); [Time, "The Lesson of Ben Het"](https://time.com/archive/6637066/world-the-lesson-of-ben-het). See also Letter 025 notes, "News reports at home".
- **Historical context (enemy strength):** In May–July 1969 the North Vietnamese **28th and 66th Infantry Regiments**, supported by the **40th Artillery Regiment**, besieged Ben Het and Dak To. The 1/92nd Artillery's operational report speaks of "two NVA Infantry Regiments and major portions of an NVA Artillery Regiment" south of Ben Het, FSB 6 and Dak To, and says that in June Ben Het "was surrounded by a large number of well-armed and well dug-in NVA". A veterans' history puts the force at **some 3,000 North Vietnamese troops**. *Sources:* [1/92nd Field Artillery Association, "Ben Het / Dak To, May–July 1969"](https://bravecannons.com/History/hist_benhet.html) (link from Andy); [Vietnam Helicopter Pilots Association, "Siege of Ben Het and Dak To CIDG camps", 5 May – 29 June 1969](https://mail.vhpa.org/KIA/panel/battle/69050520.HTM); [Wikipedia, "Ben Het Camp"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Het_Camp); [SOFREP, "Battle for Ben Het Special Forces A-Camp, June 23, 1969"](https://sofrep.com/specialoperations/battle-ben-het-special-forces-camp-june-231969/). The exact number is **unknown**; the figures above are estimates from these sources.
- **Historical context:** The monsoon reached the area in June 1969, with morning fog and afternoon thunderstorms (Project CHECO, *The Siege of Ben Het*, 1969, [DTIC ADA487237](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA487237.pdf)); compare "its pouring out now".
- **Historical context:** The enemy lifted the siege and withdrew on about 29 June 1969, and it was declared over on 2 July (same CHECO report). Dad's letter was written about ten days before.
- **Unknown:** Which bunker or position at Ben Het he was in, and whether it was with A Battery, 3/6 Artillery (Letter 026 says he was going to help A Battery). What he meant by an "old French" bunker is not known; nothing has been checked.
- **Historical context (NARA, 2026-11-10):** The 52nd Artillery Group's intelligence put the enemy around Ben Het at the 66th NVA Regiment (about 1,300), the 28th (about 1,700) and the 40th Artillery Regiment (about 1,600), plus about 500 in headquarters and support. The Army Command Historian summed it up as "three enemy regiments with an estimated strength of 5100". That is within the 3,000–6,000 Andy remembers Dad saying. Highway 512 to Ben Het was closed for days at a time in mid-June, and between 16 and 23 June supplies came only by air-drop, which fits the water "rationed a helmet full a day" (interpretation). See `research/ben-het-1969-records.md`.
