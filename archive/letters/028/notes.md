# Letter 028: notes

## Source

Two sheets of lined notebook paper (blue rules) written in black ink, three pages. Only page 2 is numbered ("2" in a circle). Addressed "Dear Dad, Mom & Kids" and dated in the text "today is April 8th". Signed "Love & miss, Tom", with a postscript below.

The plain, linen-textured envelope (flap open) is addressed in his hand to the "Jafuta Family" in West Allis, Wisconsin, with "Free" and the return address "T. Jafuta, HHB 3/6 ARTY, APO SAN FRAN. 96318". There is no postmark and a round stain near the name. Only the front was scanned.

## Open questions for the family

1. **The year.** "April 8th". He is waiting to hear "from that school" so he can get out of Vietnam and the army early, the same school early-out as Letters 019 (24 April), 009 (5 May) and 008 (May 10). So this is very likely **8 April 1969** (interpretation). Confirm?
2. **Cam Ranh Bay.** "We got back to the hill yesterday… You should have gotten my last letter & pictures by now. I mailed them down in Cam Rahn Bay." He had been away somewhere with "too much sleep" ("it was worth it"). Was this an in-country R&R at Cam Ranh Bay? Do the pictures he mailed from there survive?
3. **"[possible reading: As] you know, we never left country yet."** This seems to say that the trip was within Vietnam, not out of the country (interpretation). His out-of-country R&R (Letter 017: "Hawaii (both times)") was perhaps still ahead.
4. **"The hill."** Which hill was "the hill" in April 1969? (In Letter 005, in June, "our hill" is near Dak To.)
5. **"About 4 letters (1 from Dad) and a package."** What was in the package?
6. **Jim.** "I'd like to drop a line to Jim tonight." Jim Theilke (see Letters 002–004, 006, 008, 011)?
7. **Readings to check:** "[possible reading: As] you know" (page 1); "we never left country yet"; "Cam Rahn Bay"; the crossed-out word on page 2.

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** He mailed his last letter and pictures "down in Cam Rahn Bay" and returned "to the hill" the day before he wrote. More new men were arriving, giving the old hands "a whole 2 nights off" from guard.
- **Historical context:** Cam Ranh Bay, on the coast of South Vietnam, was a major U.S. base. Soldiers could get short in-country rest passes to coastal centers such as Vung Tau, China Beach and Cam Ranh Bay, which were usually easier to get than week-long R&R trips out of the country. *Sources:* [Wikipedia, "Cam Ranh Bay"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cam_Ranh_Bay); [Cherries (John Podlaski), "Special Services in Vietnam"](https://cherrieswriter.com/2015/03/12/special-services-in-vietnam/).
- **Interpretation:** "Too much sleep" and "it was worth it" fit a few days of rest at Cam Ranh Bay; the letter does not say why he was there.
- **Unknown:** Where "the hill" was.
