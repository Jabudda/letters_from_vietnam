# Letter 011: notes

## Source

Two sheets of thin airmail paper written in black ink, three pages. Only page 2 is numbered ("2" in a circle). Addressed "Dear Dad, Mom & Kids" and dated in the text "Today is Aug. 4". Signed "Love & miss all of you, Tom", with a decorative flourish below it. Below that he wrote "16 days" with a flourish underneath.

The airmail envelope is addressed in his hand, in black ink, to the "Jafuta Family" in West Allis, Wisconsin. It has "Free" where a stamp would go and the return address "T. Jafuta, HHB 3/6 ARTY, APO SAN FRAN 96318". There is no postmark. The back carries notes written sideways, apparently in another hand or hands: in pencil, "Polaski Hall" with a phone number, underlined; in blue ink, "#[number withheld] / Sugar / grad. South '57". These look like notes the family made later on the envelope (interpretation).

## Open questions for the family

1. **The year.** "Today is Aug. 4", "my port call is Aug. 17", "16 days". Letter 010 (24 June, very likely 1969) counted 57 days to go; both point to the same day, about **20 August**. So this is very likely **4 August 1969**, near the end of his second tour (interpretation). Can the family confirm when he came home?
2. **Jim's letter.** "Got a letter from Jim yesterday. He too thinks we made a mistake coming back." Jim had gone back for a second tour too. Is this Jim Theilke (Letters 006 and 008)?
3. ~~**"Mike or Petey."**~~ Answered: Mike is Dad's brother Michael, and Petey is also his brother (Andy, 2026-11-10). The reading "Mike" is now likely.
4. **The notes on the envelope back.** Who wrote "Polaski Hall" and the phone number (in pencil), and "#[number withheld] / Sugar / grad. South '57" (in blue ink)? Andy says Polaski Hall is where Kathy and Ed held their wedding reception, so the pencil note is very likely about booking it (interpretation). Does "grad. South '57" mean someone graduated from South Division High School in 1957? These are guesses; nothing on the envelope explains them. The phone number is withheld.
5. **"16 days."** He wrote it below his signature. Did he count down at the end of every letter?
6. **Coming home.** He would leave his unit "about the 13th or 14th" and his port call was "Aug. 17, at 11:00 at nite". Does the family remember the day he got home?
7. **Privacy.** He writes openly about his nerves, doubt, prayer and "why me in a place like this?". It is his voice; the family decides how it is published.
8. **Readings to check:** "But its nearer to an end" (page 1); "Mike" (page 2); "Oh yeah, my port call" (page 3); "Polaski Hall" and "Sugar" on the envelope back; the decorative flourish under his signature.

## Historical notes

- **Verified fact (from the letter):** His port call (his departure for the United States) was "Aug. 17, at 11:00 at nite", and he expected to leave his unit "about the 13th or 14th". He wrote "16 days" at the end.
- **Verified fact (from Letters 005 and 010):** He counted 60 days to go on 19 or 20 June and 57 days on 24 June. Counting forward from both, and from 4 August with 16 days, gives about 20 August as "the day".
- **Interpretation:** The difference between the 17 August port call and the 20 August date may be travel time home, or a count to his expected discharge. The letter does not say.
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "Got a letter from Jim yesterday. He too thinks we made a mistake coming back." He and Jim had both returned for a second tour (see Letter 008: "Jim & I were one of their first replacements... we did in '67").
- **Verified fact (from the letter):** "This place & even just the army taught me so many things. It was such a hard way to learn though."
- **Historical context:** Soldiers leaving Vietnam at the end of a tour were given a "port call", the date and time of their flight out (see Letter 007 notes on the Oakland Army Base, through which many returning soldiers passed). Where his flight left from is not stated.
- **Unknown:** Where he was when he wrote ("the Eastern Front" is his joke, not a place), and who wrote the notes on the envelope back. (Polaski Hall itself is where Kathy and Ed held their wedding reception, per Andy.)
