# Ben Het and Dak To, May–June 1969: official records

What the Army's own award files say about Operation Dan Quyen (the fight around Dak To and Ben Het, 4 May – 28 June 1969), and how it lines up with Dad's letters. Read on 2026-11-10 from scans in the National Archives Catalog (Record Group 472). The text was read by machine (OCR) from the page images, so figures should be checked against the scans before they are quoted in the book.

**The rule still holds.** These records describe units, not Dad. Nothing below places him at any particular action. Where his letters and the records touch, that is said and labelled.

## What the records say

**Historical context** (source 1 unless noted):

- **The award.** General Orders 5022 gave the Valorous Unit Award to the 52nd Artillery Group headquarters battery "and its assigned and attached units", for Dak To and Ben Het, 4 May – 28 June 1969, Operation Dan Quyen. The units named include **Battery A, 3d Battalion, 6th Artillery**. No other part of the 3rd Battalion is named. The Army's streamer reads "DAK TO – BEN HET".
- **What was asked for.** The 52nd Group recommended a Presidential Unit Citation that named "the 3d Battalion, 6th Artillery" as a whole. The Command Historian advised cutting this to the elements actually at Ben Het and Dak To. He wrote that "the 3/6 had only Btry 'A' in support". The awards board then downgraded the award to the Valorous Unit Award.
- **Where the battalion's batteries were.** The Command Historian took these locations from the unit reports:
  - Battery A was at Ben Het and Dak To, under 1/92 Artillery from April to 10 June 1969 and under 6/14 Artillery from 10 June to 20 July.
  - Battery B defended Pleiku from Artillery Hill until 6 June, then supported 1-10 Cavalry at LZ Diane/Duc Co.
  - Battery C was at LZ Diane/Duc Co until 6 June, then on Pleiku defence.
  - The battalion headquarters and command post were on Artillery Hill, Pleiku.
- **Headquarters men went forward anyway.** The narrative says the headquarters and service batteries of the three battalions were "ostensibly" at Artillery Hill, "however, the majority of their time and labors were devoted to Operation Dan Quyen". It names Headquarters Battery, 3rd Battalion, 6th Artillery men in the convoys on Highway 512 ("Suicide Run"). On 16 June a B40 rocket seriously wounded the battalion's communications officer, who was leading a convoy out of Ben Het. An enlisted man of Headquarters Battery carried him to safety, and a Headquarters NCO kept five ambushed trucks moving. The battalion's motor officer was sent to Dak To on 20 June.
- **Battery A at Ben Het.** Battery A was on the North Hill at Ben Het (Fire Support Base 12) from 1 May. Ben Het is three hills (North, Center and West) with the Special Forces camp on the West Hill.
- **The night of 23 June.** "Eight sappers, each carrying satchel charges and 4 to 5 grenades, assaulted the perimeter of Battery A, 3d Battalion, 6th Artillery at 0315 hours." Seven were killed. One satchel charge went off between howitzer sections 1 and 2, and a grenade thrown into a perimeter bunker killed one man and wounded two. The 52nd Group's chaplain spotted the eighth sapper and helped carry the wounded. The same day the NVA gave the battle the code name "Dien Bien Phu".
- **The siege.**
  - Highway 512 was closed for four days before 16 June. After the convoy of 16 June, no convoy reached Ben Het until 23 June. In between, "only essential items could be resupplied by air-drop".
  - Between 23 and 26 June, Ben Het took 434 rounds of indirect fire. The 52nd Group's units there had 3 killed and 25 wounded in those days.
  - On 27 June bombing within 500 metres of the perimeter ("combat sky spots") and massed artillery drove the enemy off. The NVA withdrew on 27–28 June.
- **The size of the enemy force.** The Group's intelligence estimated:

  | Unit | Battalions | Strength |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 66th NVA Regiment | 3 | about 1,300 |
  | 28th NVA Regiment | 3 | about 1,700 |
  | 40th NVA Artillery Regiment | 5 | about 1,600 |
  | Headquarters and support | | about 500 |

  The Command Historian sums this up as "three enemy regiments with an estimated strength of 5100".
- **Totals for the operation.**
  - The 52nd Group fired 88,205 rounds.
  - The enemy fired 3,837 rounds at the defenders.
  - The award recommendation counted 20 killed and 145 wounded in the recommended units. The Historian gives 165 casualties among the artillerymen, "approximately 8%".
  - Enemy dead credited to artillery and air were 1,754.
  - Nine individual valor awards went to men of the 3rd Battalion, 6th Artillery for the period from 1 May 1969. The end date is unclear in the scan.
- **Washington was watching.** The President's Daily Brief for 28 June 1969 reported that the Communists "stepped up their shelling of Ben Het on the night of 26 June" and hoped "to increase the pressure still further" (source 3).
- **LZ Oasis, 1968.** In 1968 the battalion ran a forward tactical command post at LZ Oasis, west of Pleiku, alongside its operations center on Artillery Hill. The battalion command post moved to the Oasis on 8 July 1968 (source 2).

## How this lines up with the letters

- **Verified fact (letters):** Dad's return address in early 1969 was "HHB 3/6 ARTY" (Letters 004, 033). He wrote "We're still up in Ben Het yet" on 18 June (034). In 047 he wrote "up in Ben Het its terrible", that "A baty almost got over run", and that "(Acoff) died".
- **Interpretation:** In 047 (about 28 June) Dad wrote that Battery A "almost got over run 2 days ago again". The records describe two matches.
  - On 23 June sappers broke into Battery A's perimeter. That is the same day Eddie Duane Acuff died (Virtual Wall).
  - NVA ground forces stayed close around Ben Het until 27 June, when bombing near the perimeter and massed artillery drove them off.

  Dad's "2 days ago" points to about 26 June, and "again" suggests the attack of 23 June came before it. Which night he means is **unknown**. The records do not name the man killed in the bunker on 23 June, so whether it was Eddie is also **unknown**.
- **Interpretation:** The records say men of Headquarters Battery went forward to Ben Het and Dak To and ran the convoys. That fits Dad being "up in Ben Het" while his battalion's headquarters was officially on Artillery Hill. It does **not** show what he did there, and he is not one of the men the narrative describes.
- **Verified fact (family):** Andy remembers Dad saying the NVA around Ben Het numbered roughly 3,000 to 6,000. The Army's estimate of about 5,100 falls within that range.
- **Not established:** The Valorous Unit Award names Battery A only. Whether Dad was on Battery A's roll in May–June 1969 is unknown.
  - Dad's own words point that way: "air lift ammo & food to our baty" and "Even our BC from A baty" (047), and ammunition "air lifted to Ben Het to our Battery" (025).
  - His return address in early 1969, though, was HHB 3/6 (interpretation).
  - His personnel file (OMPF) would show his assignments, and whether he was credited with the unit award.
- **Interpretation:** Letter 002 ("out here at the Oasis", January 1969) and Letter 037 ("LZ Oasis") match the battalion's forward post at LZ Oasis described in the 1968 recommendation.
