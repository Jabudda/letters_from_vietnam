# Army Commendation Medal: certificate and citation

Two documents Andy found and shared on 2026-11-25, as phone screenshots (archive records P011 and P012). The number after Dad's name on both is his Social Security number. Andy blacked it out before sharing, and it stays out of the archive, the book and the site (`docs/privacy.md`). When the originals are scanned, any web image must cover the same spot. A full-resolution scan is still needed for the printed book.

Published with Andy's approval (2026-11-25: "We can add both to the book and website"): on the homepage under "His journey", and in the book as "His Army papers".

## The documents

**1. Certificate (printed form, filled in by typewriter). P011.**
- Verified fact: "Department of the Army. This is to certify that the Secretary of the Army has awarded the Army Commendation Medal to Specialist Four Thomas Jafuta, [number withheld], United States Army, for meritorious achievement in the Republic of Vietnam during the period June 1969 to July 1969."
- Verified fact: "Given under my hand in the city of Washington this thirtieth day of August 1969."
- Verified fact: Signed by Winant Sidle, Brigadier General, USA, and by Stanley R. Resor, Secretary of the Army.
- Handwritten at top right: "Please return to me. Thank you." Verified fact (family): this is Dad's handwriting (Andy, 2026-11-25).

**2. Citation (typed sheet). P012.**
- Verified fact: "The Army Commendation Medal is awarded to Specialist Four Thomas P. Jafuta, [number withheld], United States Army, for meritorious service."
- Verified fact: The citation says he "distinguished himself by exceptionally meritorious service in the Republic of Vietnam during the period September 1968 through August 1969, while serving as senior field wireman with Headquarters and Headquarters Battery, 3d Battalion, 6th Artillery."
- Verified fact: It credits him with three things:
  - installing and maintaining the wiring in the battalion base camp area, "reliable insurance against broken communications during emergency situations";
  - wiring "a new tactical operations center at Landing Zone Oasis";
  - instructing replacement personnel in the communications section.
- Handwritten at right: "Original copy. Please return." Verified fact (family): this is Dad's handwriting (Andy, 2026-11-25).
- The full text, as typed, is in `book/text/service.md`. It was read from the screenshot (Claude, 2026-11-25) and is for family check.

## What this settles

- **His job.** Verified fact: Senior field wireman, HHB 3/6 Artillery. This answers the open question in Letter 004's notes ("was he a wireman or in communications?"). It fits his own words: fixing "a bunker line" and getting a shock (004), and "Had to fix a line before. It seems everytime a line goes out its at night" (032).
- **Rank and full name.** Verified fact: Specialist Four (SP4) Thomas P. Jafuta.
- **Unit.** Verified fact: HHB, 3rd Battalion, 6th Artillery. This matches his return address and the family's confirmation.
- **The Oasis.** Verified fact: he wired a new TOC at LZ Oasis. This fits Letters 002 ("out here at the Oasis"), 032 and 037 ("LZ Oasis"). Historical context: the battalion ran a forward command post at LZ Oasis from July 1968 (`research/ben-het-1969-records.md`, source 2).
- **The dates of this tour.** Verified fact: the citation covers September 1968 through August 1969 in Vietnam.
- **Eddie Acuff.** Verified fact (family): Andy recalls that Eddie Acuff ("Acoff" in the letters) and Dad were in the same unit (2026-11-25). The records agree. Dad's citation puts him in Headquarters and Headquarters Battery, 3d Battalion, 6th Artillery, September 1968 – August 1969, and the Virtual Wall gives SP4 Eddie Duane Acuff's unit as "HHB, 3RD BN, 6TH ARTILLERY, 52ND ARTY GROUP, I FIELD FORCE, USARV" (https://www.virtualwall.org/da/AcuffED01a.htm), killed on 23 June 1969. So they were in the same battery. On the day he died, though, Eddie was serving with Battery A: his Silver Star citation says he had volunteered from HHB's survey section to fill shortages there (see `research/ben-het-1969-records.md`). Nothing here says where Dad was that day, or places him with Eddie when he was killed.
- **A second award.** Verified fact (family): Dad received a second Army Commendation Medal (Andy, 2026-11-25). Its certificate or citation has not been found yet; Andy is still searching.

| The award itself | Individual Army Commendation Medals were announced in General Orders of the awarding headquarters. A search of the National Archives Catalog (2026-11-25) found only unit-award files for these units, no individual award orders. | **Not found online.** The OMPF normally holds a copy of each award order, with its number and date. |

**Historical context, not linked to Dad:** LZ Oasis came under a major night attack on 10–11 May 1969. One veteran's account from another unit there says a field-telephone cable from the fire direction center "got hit and cut in two" (*The Mighty Ninth*, San Diego State University, "Happy Mother's Day"). It does not mention the 3d Battalion, 6th Artillery. Letter 003 ("We had to go to the Oasis yesterday and some bad thing happened") already has its own notes on this attack.

**Where else to look.** The 3d Battalion, 6th Artillery's own quarterly reports (Operational Reports – Lessons Learned) for the periods ending 31 January, 30 April and 31 July 1969 would be the best unit record for the new TOC. They were not found online. Copies may be at the National Archives at College Park (with the 52d Artillery Group report for the period ending 31 July 1969, NAID 7332649, box 156) or in the Morris Swett Library at Fort Sill, whose catalog lists the battalion's reports from 1966 (DTIC ADA112018).

Sources: DTIC AD0505158 (https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/AD0505158.pdf); NARA NAID 167327485 (https://catalog.archives.gov/id/167327485), read from the page images by machine (OCR) on 2026-11-25; 1/92d Field Artillery Association, ORLL May–July 1969 (https://www.bravecannons.org/History/ORLL/j_may69-jul69.html); Center of Military History, appendix B (https://webdoc.sub.gwdg.de/ebook/p/2005/CMH_2/www.army.mil/cmh-pg/books/sw-sa/app-b.htm); The Mighty Ninth (https://themightyninth.sdsu.edu/War%20Stories/Happy%20Mothers%20Day/Happy%20Mother's%20Day.htm).

## Open points (not to be guessed)

1. **Which document belongs to which award.** The certificate says "meritorious achievement", June–July 1969. The citation says "meritorious service", September 1968 – August 1969. The Army normally uses these wordings for separate awards, which fits the family's word that there were two. Until the second set of papers turns up, which certificate goes with which citation is **unknown**. The missing pieces are probably a "service" certificate for September 1968 – August 1969 and an "achievement" citation for June–July 1969. The medal case (a second medal, or an oak leaf cluster on the ribbon), his personnel file (OMPF) or the claim file in point 5 would confirm it.
2. **June–July 1969.** That period overlaps the siege of Ben Het. **Nothing in the certificate links the award to Ben Het or to any action**, and none should be implied (CLAUDE.md rule 5). If the achievement citation turns up, it will say what the award was for.
3. **Historical context:** Brig. Gen. Winant Sidle, who signed the certificate, commanded I Field Force Vietnam Artillery from 16 March to 10 September 1969 (see the cross-check above). He also endorsed the 52nd Artillery Group's Ben Het award recommendation (`research/ben-het-1969-records.md`, source 1). That is his position, not a link to Dad's award.
4. **Two tours.** The family says Dad served two tours. This citation covers one twelve-month period, starting in September 1968. (The archive has dated the second tour from October 1968, from the first letters; the citation's start date is a month earlier. To check with the family.) The other tour's dates are **unknown** from these documents.
5. ~~The handwriting~~ **Answered:** Dad wrote "Please return" on both when he sent the papers in support of a claim late in his life (Andy, 2026-11-25). The details are private and kept in `private/family.md`. Copies he sent may survive in that claim file, which could also hold the second award's papers.
