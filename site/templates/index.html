01 / Why we’re keeping them

History has dates.
These letters have a voice.

War is more than dates, places, and battles. It is sons, brothers, friends, parents, fear, hope, and survival. These letters offer a glimpse of that human experience through one family’s correspondence.

Dad’s Vietnam Letter Archive brings together letters kept by his family, with original scans and carefully prepared transcriptions as the collection takes shape.

These letters are shared as accurately as we can make them: his spelling, his humor, his worries, just as he wrote them. We keep them as a reminder to people in the future that war does not end when a soldier comes home. Its effects reach far and last long, for the family who waited and wrote back, and for the man who went.

Stories Dad told

Now I Know What They Mean

Ben Het, during the 1969 siege · Dad’s story, as Andy remembers it

Mortars were coming in, and Dad was running across the battlefield when one landed really close. He pooped his pants, and then he started laughing as he ran. He jumped into the closest foxhole, and the guys stared at him, bewildered: he had nearly lost his life, and he was laughing. Then he told them, and the smell told them the rest.

“Now I know what they mean,” he said, “about having the shit scared out of you.”

Dad rarely swore. — Andy

Good Citizens

Milwaukee, 1976 · newspaper clipping, family photograph and Andy’s account

Back home, Dad and his friend Ronnie caught two men stealing gas from Dad’s car and “detained” them until the police came. Both received citizen awards of merit from the Milwaukee Police Department. Mom (Laura) and Bonnie, Ronnie’s wife, accepted the awards for them, because Dad and Ronnie were “committed to their jobs and paying the bills.”

Preserve the page.
Respect the voice.

01

The original comes first

Scans preserve the physical letters as the primary source, alongside readable transcriptions.

02

Careful, faithful reading

Dad’s wording and character belong to the story. Unclear handwriting is marked for review, never guessed.

03

Context, kept separate

Family memories and editorial notes help explain the letters without becoming part of the original text.
