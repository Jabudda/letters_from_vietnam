# Where he was, and what was happening

*A short background. Each statement is labeled; the sources are listed at the back of the book.*

**His unit.** Dad’s return address reads “HHB 3/6 ARTY”: Headquarters and Headquarters Battery, 3rd Battalion, 6th Artillery. *(Fact, from his envelopes.)* The battalion belonged to the 52nd Artillery Group, part of I Field Force Vietnam, and its headquarters was on Artillery Hill outside Pleiku, in the Central Highlands. *(Historical context: Army records.)* Its batteries moved between firebases around Pleiku, among them LZ Oasis, which he mentions often. *(Historical context.)*

**Two tours.** Dad served two tours in Vietnam. He went back for the second, the family remembers, to be with his friends, and then found they were all going home. *(Family.)* The letters in this book come from both tours. *(Family.)* Those he dated with a year, or that carry a postmark, fall between October 1968 and August 1969. *(Fact, from the letters.)*

**Ben Het and Dak To, 1969.** Ben Het was a Special Forces camp in the far northwest of the Central Highlands, near where Vietnam, Laos and Cambodia meet. It was held by a small team of US Special Forces with Montagnard soldiers (and their families), South Vietnamese troops and American artillery. *(Historical context.)* From 4 May to 28 June 1969, in what the Army called Operation Dan Quyen, North Vietnamese Army regiments attacked Dak To and Ben Het. The Army estimated the enemy at three regiments, about 5,100 men. Highway 512 between Dak To and Ben Het was cut for days at a time, and for a week in late June the camp, surrounded, was supplied only by air. *(Historical context: the 52nd Artillery Group’s records.)* Battery A of Dad’s battalion held the North Hill at Ben Het through the siege. *(Historical context: Army records.)* Dad’s own letters put him “up in Ben Het” in mid-June and at Dak To, helping to airlift ammunition, at the end of it. *(Fact, from the letters.)*

**Going home.** By June 1969 his early release for school had been approved, and his date to leave the army was set for 21 August. On about 8 August he wrote what he called “the last letter I’ll be writing”, with “about 8 days left in the army”. *(Fact, from the letters.)*
